This note covers syllabus items **3.4 Money Laundering Prevention Act, 2064**
and **3.5 Banking Offence and Punishment Act, 2064**. Both were passed in
**2064 BS (2008)**, and both deal with **crime involving banks**, but from
different angles:

- The **money laundering law** stops **criminal money** from moving through
  the financial system.
- The **banking offence law** punishes **fraud and abuse against banks and
  their customers**.

## Assets (Money) Laundering Prevention Act, 2064

### What is money laundering?

**Money laundering** is making money that came from **crime** look as if it
came from a **legitimate source**. It usually happens in **three stages**:

| Stage | What happens | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Placement** | Criminal cash enters the financial system | Depositing cash in many small amounts |
| **Layering** | The money's origin is hidden through many transactions | Moving funds between accounts, companies and countries |
| **Integration** | The money comes back looking clean | Buying property or a business |

**Terrorist financing** means providing or collecting funds for terrorism. The
money may even come from legal sources. The Act covers both.

### Main provisions

- **Offences.** The Act makes **money laundering** and **terrorist financing**
  crimes. It also lists the **predicate offences**: the underlying crimes,
  such as **corruption, tax evasion, drug and human trafficking, fraud,
  smuggling and organised crime**, whose proceeds cannot be laundered.
- **Reporting entities.** Banks and financial institutions, insurers,
  securities firms, casinos, real-estate dealers, lawyers, accountants and
  others must carry out **KYC and customer due diligence**, **keep records**
  and **report** to FIU-Nepal.
- **FIU-Nepal.** The **Financial Information Unit**, set up within **NRB**,
  receives **threshold transaction reports (TTRs)** and **suspicious
  transaction reports (STRs)**, analyses them, and passes intelligence to
  investigators.
- **Investigation.** The **Department of Money Laundering Investigation
  (DMLI)** investigates and prosecutes money laundering cases.
- **Regulators.** Each sector's regulator supervises AML/CFT compliance: **NRB**
  for BFIs, **SEBON** for securities and the **Nepal Insurance Authority** for
  insurers.
- **Freezing and confiscation.** Suspected criminal property can be
  **frozen**, and the proceeds of crime can be **confiscated**.
- **Penalties.** Offenders face **imprisonment and fines**, and the property
  involved is **confiscated**. Reporting entities that fail in their duties can
  also be **penalised**.

For the day-to-day bank duties under this Act, such as KYC, CDD, TTR, STR and
the rule against tipping off, see
[KYC, Audit and Internal Control](/docs/kyc-audit-internal-control).

### International context

- Nepal is a member of the **Asia/Pacific Group on Money Laundering (APG)**.
  The APG assesses Nepal against the **FATF** standards.
- In **February 2025**, the **FATF** placed Nepal on its **grey list**
  (increased monitoring). Nepal must **fix the weaknesses** FATF identified to
  be removed from the list.

## Banking Offence and Punishment Act, 2064

### Purpose

The **Banking Offence and Punishment Act, 2064 (2008)** keeps the **banking
system credible** by defining **banking offences** and setting **punishments**
for them. It covers offences by **customers, borrowers, outsiders** and **bank
staff and officials**.

### Main banking offences

| Category | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| **Account and instrument fraud** | Opening an account in a **false name** or with fake documents; **forging** cheques, drafts, cards or other instruments; withdrawing money **without authority** |
| **Cheque offences** | **Issuing a cheque without enough balance** in the account, knowing it will bounce |
| **Loan offences** | Getting a loan with **false documents** or **overvalued or fake collateral**; **using a loan for a different purpose** than approved; taking a loan and **not using it as agreed** |
| **Collusion by staff** | Bank staff **approving loans improperly**, **valuing collateral falsely** or misusing their position, alone or with others |
| **Electronic and card fraud** | **Card skimming or cloning**, unauthorised electronic transfers, misuse of a customer's credentials |
| **Other** | Misusing **deposits**, giving false information to the bank, and **abetting or attempting** any banking offence |

### Investigation and punishment

- **Nepal Police** investigates banking offences, and the **Government
  Attorney** prosecutes them.
- The court can impose a **fine** and **imprisonment**, both **scaled to the
  amount involved**, and order **recovery** of the claimed amount from the
  offender.
- **Bank officials** involved in an offence are punished **as well as** the
  outside offenders. Their position can count against them.

> [!NOTE]
> A **bounced cheque** can lead to action under **both** this Act and the
> **[Negotiable Instruments Act, 2034](/docs/negotiable-instruments-act-2034)**.

## Comparing the two Acts

| Point | Money Laundering Prevention Act | Banking Offence and Punishment Act |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Year | 2064 (2008) | 2064 (2008) |
| Targets | Criminal money passing **through** the financial system | Fraud and abuse **against** banks and their customers |
| Key bodies | FIU-Nepal (in NRB), DMLI, sector regulators | Nepal Police, Government Attorney, courts |
| Main duty on banks | KYC, CDD, record-keeping, TTR and STR reporting | Strong internal control; reporting offences |
| Typical case | Hiding bribe money through layered transfers | Taking a loan on fake collateral |

## Quick revision

> [!TIP]
> **One-line answers.** Both Acts date from **2064 (2008)**. Laundering has
> three stages: **placement, layering, integration**. **FIU-Nepal** sits within
> **NRB**; the **DMLI** investigates laundering. Nepal is an **APG** member and
> was **FATF grey-listed in February 2025**. Banking offences include a **false
> account**, **forgery**, a **cheque without balance**, **loans on false
> documents or collateral**, **misuse of a loan** and **collusion by staff**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Traps that show up in multiple-choice questions: terrorist financing can use
> **legally earned** money; the **FIU** analyses reports but does **not**
> prosecute; **layering** is the stage that **hides** the origin;
> **misusing a loan** is itself a banking offence; **bank staff** can be
> punished under the Banking Offence Act too.
