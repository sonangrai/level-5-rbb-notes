**Non-bank financial institutions (NBFIs)** collect and invest money without
being banks. NRB does not license them, and most of them do not take ordinary
deposits. Their money comes from **insurance premiums** or from **compulsory
or voluntary long-term contributions**. That makes them Nepal's main source of
**long-term funds** for government bonds, infrastructure and the capital
market.

This note covers the four NBFIs in the syllabus:

| NBFI | Nepali name | Governing law | Regulator / oversight |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Insurance companies | बीमा कम्पनी | Insurance Act, 2079 | **Nepal Insurance Authority** |
| Employees Provident Fund | कर्मचारी सञ्चय कोष | Employees Provident Fund Act, 2019 | Ministry of Finance |
| Citizen Investment Trust | नागरिक लगानी कोष | Citizen Investment Trust Act, 2047 | Ministry of Finance |
| Social Security Fund | सामाजिक सुरक्षा कोष | Contribution-Based Social Security Act, 2074 | Ministry of Labour, Employment and Social Security |

## Insurance companies

Insurance **transfers risk**. Many people each pay a small **premium** into a
common pool, and the pool pays the few who suffer a loss.

### Types

| Type | Covers |
| --- | --- |
| **Life insurance** | Death, endowment, term, child and pension plans |
| **Non-life (general) insurance** | Property, fire, motor, marine, health, engineering, aviation, agriculture and livestock |
| **Microinsurance** | Low-cost life and non-life cover for low-income people |
| **Reinsurance** | Insurance for insurance companies (Nepal Re, Himalayan Re) |

### Regulation

- The regulator used to be the **Insurance Board (बीमा समिति)**. Under the
  **Insurance Act, 2079 (2022)** it became the **Nepal Insurance Authority
  (नेपाल बीमा प्राधिकरण)**.
- The Authority issues licences, sets **minimum capital and solvency rules**,
  approves insurance products and protects policyholders.
- Like the BFIs, the industry went through a **merger drive** after capital
  requirements were raised. Life and non-life insurers each fell to about
  **14 companies**.

### Role and contribution

- **Protection**: insurance covers individuals and businesses against death,
  illness, accidents and damage to property.
- **Long-term savings**: life insurance premiums go into bank fixed deposits,
  government bonds and shares.
- **Support for lending**: banks require insurance on collateral and on loans,
  which makes lending safer.
- **Social protection**: crop and livestock insurance (with a government
  premium subsidy) and the government's **health insurance programme** reach
  vulnerable groups.

### Status

- **Insurance penetration is low.** Life insurance coverage has grown fast,
  but many people still have no non-life or health cover.
- Recent issues include **slow claim settlement**, **mis-selling by agents**,
  and pressure on insurers to keep enough solvency capital after mergers.

## Employees Provident Fund (कर्मचारी सञ्चय कोष)

The **Employees Provident Fund (EPF)**, also called **Karmachari Sanchaya
Kosh**, was set up in **2019 BS (1962)** under the **Employees Provident Fund
Act, 2019**. It is Nepal's **oldest and largest** retirement savings fund.

### How it works

- Every month the employee contributes **10% of basic salary** and the employer
  adds **10%**, so **20%** goes into the fund.
- Membership is **compulsory** for civil servants, the army, police, teachers
  and employees of public enterprises. Private employers can also join.
- The member receives the whole balance, **with interest**, on retirement or
  on leaving the job.

### Role and contribution

- **Retirement security** for millions of formal-sector workers.
- **Loans to its own members**: housing, education, special and emergency
  loans at fair interest rates.
- **Investment in the economy**: it puts money into **hydropower and other
  infrastructure** (often together with banks), buys government bonds and
  holds bank deposits.
- Its pool of **long-term money** helps the government finance big projects
  that need patient capital.

### Status

- EPF is one of the **largest financial institutions in Nepal** by fund size.
- Recent debates are about **returns to members**, how much **risk** its
  infrastructure investments carry, and how it fits alongside the newer
  **Social Security Fund**.

## Citizen Investment Trust (नागरिक लगानी कोष)

The **Citizen Investment Trust (CIT)**, or **Nagarik Lagani Kosh**, was
established in **2047 BS (1991)** under the **Citizen Investment Trust Act,
2047**. Its aim is to **encourage ordinary citizens to save and invest** and
to **widen ownership of the capital market**.

### Schemes and services

| Scheme / service | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Employees' Savings Growth Scheme** (कर्मचारी बचत वृद्धि कोष) | Voluntary retirement savings for employees, with tax benefits |
| **Pension and gratuity management** | Manages **pension and gratuity funds** for teachers and for employees of organisations that hand them over |
| **Investors' Account Scheme** | Members invest small amounts regularly |
| **Unit schemes / mutual funds** | Pooled investment in shares and bonds |
| **Insurance scheme** | Insurance cover attached to member savings |
| **Merchant banking** | Issue manager, underwriter and share registrar for public offerings |

### Role and contribution

- **Builds the habit of saving**, especially among salaried people, helped by
  **tax-deductible contributions**.
- **Develops the capital market**: CIT runs unit schemes, manages share issues
  and invests in shares.
- **Funds development**: it puts money into government securities and
  infrastructure.

### Status

- CIT has grown into a **major institutional investor** in Nepal's share
  market.
- Like EPF, it faces the question of how to fit alongside the **Social Security
  Fund**, and pressure to **raise returns** for members.

## Social Security Fund (सामाजिक सुरक्षा कोष)

The **Social Security Fund (SSF)** runs Nepal's **contribution-based social
security scheme** for workers. It operates under the **Contribution-Based
Social Security Act, 2074 (2017)**. The scheme was launched on **11 Mangsir
2075 (27 November 2018)**.

### Contribution

The total contribution is **31% of basic salary**:

| Paid by | Share |
| --- | --- |
| **Employee** | **11%** |
| **Employer** | **20%** |
| **Total** | **31%** |

The 31% includes what used to go to the provident fund and gratuity, so for
covered workers SSF **replaces** those older schemes.

### Schemes

1. **Medical treatment, health and maternity protection scheme**
2. **Accident and disability protection scheme**
3. **Dependent family protection scheme** (for the family if the worker dies)
4. **Old-age protection scheme** (pension)

### Coverage

- It began with **formal private-sector employees**.
- Over time it has been opened to **self-employed and informal-sector workers**
  and to **Nepalis in foreign employment**.
- It is **compulsory for private-sector employers**. Enforcement is weak,
  however, and many employers have still not enrolled.

### Role and contribution

- It is Nepal's **first comprehensive social security system** for
  private-sector workers. Before it, only government employees had pensions.
- **Protection over a whole working life**: health, accident, death and old
  age.
- It **builds a large pool of long-term savings** that can go into government
  bonds and development projects.

### Status

- Contributor numbers are rising but are still **small next to the
  workforce**.
- Recent issues include **slow employer enrolment**, **pushback from
  employers**, keeping the **pension fund sustainable over the long term**,
  and **overlap with EPF and CIT**.

## Comparing the three funds

| Point | EPF | CIT | SSF |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Established | 2019 BS (1962) | 2047 BS (1991) | Act 2074; launched 2075 BS (2018) |
| Main members | Government and public-sector staff | Voluntary savers; teachers' pensions | Private-sector and informal workers |
| Contribution | 10% + 10% | Voluntary | 11% + 20% = 31% |
| Nature | Provident fund (lump sum) | Savings and investment trust | Social insurance (pension and benefits) |
| Extra role | Member loans; infrastructure financing | Merchant banking; mutual funds | Health, accident and dependent benefits |

## Quick revision

> [!TIP]
> **One-line answers.** Insurance regulator: **Nepal Insurance Authority**
> (Insurance Act **2079**; formerly the **Insurance Board**). **EPF**: 2019 BS,
> **10% + 10%**. **CIT**: 2047 BS; savings, pensions, mutual funds and merchant
> banking. **SSF**: Act **2074**, launched **11 Mangsir 2075**,
> **11% + 20% = 31%**, **four schemes**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Traps that show up in multiple-choice questions: the SSF contribution is
> **31%, not 30%**; the **employer** pays the larger share (**20%**); NBFIs
> are **not licensed by NRB**; EPF is the **oldest** of the three funds; CIT
> also works as an **issue manager** in the share market; reinsurance means
> **insurance for insurers**.
