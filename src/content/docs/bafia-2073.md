This note covers syllabus item **3.2 Bank and Financial Institution Act,
2073**.

The **Bank and Financial Institution Act (BAFIA), 2073 (2017)** is the main
law for **banks and financial institutions (BFIs)**. It sets the rules for
how a BFI is set up and licensed, who owns and runs it, what it may and may
not do, and how it merges, fails or closes.

## Background

| Period | Law |
| --- | --- |
| Before **2063** | Separate laws for each type: **Commercial Bank Act, 2031**, **Finance Company Act, 2042**, **Development Bank Act, 2052** and others |
| **2063 (2006)** | **BAFIA, 2063**, the first **umbrella act**, which brought all BFIs under one law |
| **2073 (2017)** | **BAFIA, 2073**, which **replaced** the 2063 Act, strengthened corporate governance and made mergers easier |

## Establishment and licensing

- A BFI must be registered as a **public limited company** under the
  **Companies Act**.
- It needs a **licence from NRB** before it can carry on any financial
  business.
- NRB licenses BFIs in **four classes**: **A** (commercial banks), **B**
  (development banks), **C** (finance companies) and **D** (microfinance
  institutions). See
  [Banking Institutions in Nepal](/docs/banking-institutions-nepal).
- NRB sets the **minimum paid-up capital** for each class and the split of
  shares between **promoters** and the **public**.
- **Promoter shares** cannot be **sold or transferred** freely. A sale needs
  NRB approval and must follow NRB's rules.

## Corporate governance

### Board of directors

- There can be **no more than seven directors**.
- At least **one** must be an **independent director**: a professional with
  no shareholding or business link to the BFI.
- A person **cannot be a director of more than one BFI** at the same time.
- Directors must meet **fit-and-proper** conditions on qualification,
  experience and integrity. For example, a person who is **blacklisted** or
  has been convicted of certain offences is **disqualified**.

### Chief executive officer

- The **board** appoints the **CEO** for a term of **four years**, and may
  reappoint them.
- The CEO must meet NRB's conditions on qualification and experience.

### Board committees

- **Audit Committee**: chaired by a **non-executive director**, with the
  **head of internal audit** as secretary.
- **Risk Management Committee**.
- **Human Resource Committee**.
- **AML/CFT Committee**.

## Restrictions on operations

BAFIA forbids, or limits, transactions that are risky or that benefit
insiders:

- **No loans to insiders.** A BFI **cannot lend** to its **promoters,
  directors, CEO**, their **close family** or companies they control, except
  as the Act allows, such as loans against fixed deposits.
- **Lending limits.** NRB sets a **single-obligor limit**: the most a BFI can
  lend to one borrower or group. It also sets **sectoral limits**.
- **No insider share deals.** A BFI cannot **buy or lend against its own
  shares**.
- **Reserves before dividends.** A BFI must move **20% of its net profit** into
  the **general reserve** every year until the reserve equals **twice its
  paid-up capital**.
- **NRB approval for dividends.** A BFI can declare a dividend only after NRB
  has **approved its audited financial statements**.
- **Confidentiality.** A BFI must keep its **customers' account information
  secret**, except where the law requires disclosure, for example to a court,
  to NRB or to FIU-Nepal.

## Mergers and acquisitions

- BAFIA has a full **chapter on mergers and acquisitions**. It works together
  with NRB's merger and acquisition bylaws.
- BFIs need **NRB's approval** to merge, and **shareholders** must approve in
  a general meeting.
- After a merger, the new institution takes over **all assets, liabilities
  and staff** of the merging BFIs.
- These provisions made possible the **wave of mergers** after the
  **2015/16** increase in capital requirements.

## Problem BFIs, liquidation and depositor protection

- **NRB** can step in when a BFI becomes **weak or problematic**, using its
  powers under both BAFIA and the **NRB Act**.
- A BFI can be **liquidated** voluntarily or by order.
- In a liquidation, **depositors are paid before shareholders**.
- **Deposits are insured** by the **Deposit and Credit Guarantee Fund
  (DCGF)**, up to a set limit for each depositor in each institution.

## Quick revision

> [!TIP]
> **One-line answers.** **BAFIA, 2073** replaced **BAFIA, 2063**, which was the
> first umbrella act. BFIs are **public limited companies** licensed by
> **NRB** in classes **A–D**. The board has **at most 7** directors, including
> **at least 1 independent** director. The **CEO** serves a **4-year** term.
> **20% of net profit** goes to the general reserve until it equals **2×
> paid-up capital**. **Dividends** need **NRB approval** of the accounts.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Traps that show up in multiple-choice questions: a person may be a director
> of **only one** BFI; **promoter shares** cannot be sold freely; **loans to
> insiders** are prohibited; the **board**, not NRB, appoints the CEO, though
> NRB sets the eligibility rules; **2063** was the first unified act and
> **2073** is the current one.
