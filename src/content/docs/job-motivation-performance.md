This note covers syllabus item **4.2 Job Motivation and Performance**.

**Motivation** is the inner force that makes a person **start, direct and keep
up** effort towards a goal. At work it decides **how hard** and **how long**
people try.

Motivation alone does not produce results. Performance is often summarised as:

> **Performance = Ability × Motivation × Opportunity**

A capable person with no motivation performs poorly, and so does a motivated
person with no ability or resources.

## Types of motivation

| Type | Source | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Intrinsic** | From **inside**: the work itself | Interest, sense of achievement, pride, growth |
| **Extrinsic** | From **outside**: rewards and pressure | Salary, bonus, promotion, praise, fear of punishment |
| **Financial** | Money | Pay, incentives, allowances, profit sharing |
| **Non-financial** | Other than money | Recognition, job security, training, responsibility, a good work environment |

## Content theories: *what* motivates people

### Maslow's hierarchy of needs (1943)

People meet their needs **in order**. Once a lower need is satisfied, it no
longer motivates, and the next level up becomes the motivator.

| Level | Need | At work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5 (top) | **Self-actualisation** | Challenging work, growth, creativity |
| 4 | **Esteem** | Recognition, status, promotion, titles |
| 3 | **Social** (belonging) | Friendly colleagues, team spirit |
| 2 | **Safety** | Job security, pension, safe workplace |
| 1 (base) | **Physiological** | Basic salary, food, rest |

### Herzberg's two-factor theory (1959)

Herzberg found that **satisfaction** and **dissatisfaction** come from
**different factors**:

| Hygiene factors (prevent dissatisfaction) | Motivators (create satisfaction) |
| --- | --- |
| Salary | Achievement |
| Company policy and administration | Recognition |
| Supervision | The work itself |
| Working conditions | Responsibility |
| Relations with colleagues | Advancement |
| Job security | Growth |

**Key idea:** improving hygiene factors only **removes dissatisfaction**; it
does not **motivate**. To motivate, managers must provide the **motivators**,
for example through **job enrichment**.

### McGregor's Theory X and Theory Y (1960)

| Theory X | Theory Y |
| --- | --- |
| People **dislike work** and avoid it | Work is as **natural as play or rest** |
| They must be **controlled and threatened** | People **direct themselves** towards goals they are committed to |
| They avoid responsibility | They **seek responsibility** |
| Leads to **close supervision** | Leads to **participation and delegation** |

### McClelland's acquired needs theory

People are driven by three needs, learned through experience:

- **Need for achievement (nAch)**: to excel and succeed.
- **Need for affiliation (nAff)**: to be liked and accepted.
- **Need for power (nPow)**: to influence and control others.

### Alderfer's ERG theory

**ERG** stands for **Existence, Relatedness and Growth**. It simplifies Maslow
into three levels. Unlike Maslow, it allows **several needs at once**. It also
has a **frustration–regression** effect: if a higher need is blocked, a person
**falls back** to seeking more of a lower need.

## Process theories: *how* motivation works

### Vroom's expectancy theory (1964)

> **Motivation = Expectancy × Instrumentality × Valence**

| Term | Question the employee asks |
| --- | --- |
| **Expectancy** | "If I try harder, will I **perform** better?" |
| **Instrumentality** | "If I perform better, will I get the **reward**?" |
| **Valence** | "Do I **value** that reward?" |

If **any** factor is zero, motivation is zero.

### Adams' equity theory

Employees compare their **inputs** (effort, skill) and **outcomes** (pay,
recognition) with those of **others**. If they see **unfairness**, they act to
restore balance: by **reducing effort**, asking for more, or leaving.

### Locke's goal-setting theory

Goals that are **specific and challenging**, and that people **accept**,
produce higher performance than vague goals such as "do your best".
**Feedback** on progress strengthens the effect. Goals are often written to be
**SMART**: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant and Time-bound.

### Skinner's reinforcement theory

Behaviour that is **rewarded is repeated**, and behaviour that is **punished**
or ignored **fades**. Rewards work best when they are **timely** and clearly
linked to the behaviour.

## Ways to motivate employees

- **Fair pay** and **performance-based incentives**.
- **Recognition**: employee of the month, appreciation letters.
- **Job enrichment**: adding **responsibility and decision-making** to a job
  (vertical expansion).
- **Job enlargement**: adding **more tasks** at the same level (horizontal
  expansion).
- **Job rotation**: moving people between jobs. It builds skills and, in
  banks, also helps **detect fraud**.
- **Participation** in decisions, and **delegation**.
- **Training and career development**.
- **Good working conditions**, **work–life balance** and **job security**.

## Performance management

**Performance management** is a continuous cycle:

```text
Plan (set goals, KRAs/KPIs) → Monitor (coach, give feedback)
        ↑                                   ↓
Reward / develop  ←  Appraise (review results)
```

### Performance appraisal methods

| Method | How it works |
| --- | --- |
| **Rating scale** | The rater scores traits or results on a scale, such as 1–5 |
| **Ranking** | Employees are ranked from best to worst |
| **Forced distribution** | Fixed shares of staff must fall into each band, such as 10% top and 10% bottom |
| **Management by Objectives (MBO)** | Superior and employee **set goals together** and later review whether they were achieved |
| **360-degree feedback** | Feedback from **superiors, peers, subordinates, customers** and the employee themselves |
| **Behaviourally Anchored Rating Scale (BARS)** | Each rating level is tied to examples of specific behaviour |
| **Balanced Scorecard** | Measures performance from four angles: **financial, customer, internal process, and learning and growth** |

### Common appraisal errors

| Error | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Halo effect** | One **good** trait colours the whole rating |
| **Horns effect** | One **bad** trait colours the whole rating |
| **Central tendency** | Rating **everyone as average** |
| **Leniency / strictness** | Rating everyone **too high** or **too low** |
| **Recency effect** | Judging only by **recent** performance |
| **Personal bias** | Letting likes and dislikes affect the rating |

## In banking

- Branch and staff targets are set for **deposits, loans, recovery, NPLs,
  digital customers and fee income**. These targets must be **realistic**,
  or staff may take shortcuts with KYC or credit quality.
- **Recognition**, **career growth** and **fair transfers and promotions** are
  strong motivators, especially in state-owned banks such as RBB, where pay
  is set by rules.

## Quick revision

> [!TIP]
> **One-line answers.** **Maslow** has 5 levels: physiological, safety,
> social, esteem, self-actualisation. **Herzberg**: **hygiene** factors
> (salary, policy, supervision, conditions) prevent dissatisfaction;
> **motivators** (achievement, recognition, work itself, responsibility,
> growth) create satisfaction. **McGregor**: Theory **X** (control) and
> Theory **Y** (trust). **McClelland**: achievement, affiliation, power.
> **Vroom**: **E × I × V**. **Adams**: **equity**. **Locke**: specific,
> challenging goals.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Traps that show up in multiple-choice questions: in Herzberg's theory,
> **salary is a hygiene factor**, not a motivator; **job enrichment** is
> **vertical**, **job enlargement** is **horizontal**; **ERG** allows
> **regression**, Maslow does not; the **halo effect** is about **one good
> trait**; **MBO** sets goals **jointly**.
