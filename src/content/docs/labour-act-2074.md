This note covers syllabus item **5.2 Labour Act, 2074**.

The **Labour Act, 2074 (2017)** governs the relationship between **employers
and workers** in Nepal. It replaced the **Labour Act, 2048**, and is supported
by the **Labour Rules, 2075**. It is based on the Constitution's **right to
labour** (Article 34) and Nepal's international labour commitments.

## Main principles

- **Forced labour** is prohibited.
- **Discrimination** is prohibited, including on the grounds of gender,
  religion, caste or ethnicity. **Equal pay for equal work**.
- **Child labour** is prohibited: no one **under 14** may be employed, and
  people under 18 cannot do **hazardous work**.
- Wages are paid only for work done (**"no work, no pay"**), except during
  leave.
- **Social security** is a right. Contributions go to the **[Social Security
  Fund](/docs/non-bank-financial-institutions-nepal)**.

## Types of employment

| Type | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Regular** | Permanent work, with no fixed end date |
| **Work-based** | Employment for a **specific task**; it ends when the task is done |
| **Time-based** | Employment for a **fixed period** |
| **Casual** | Work of **up to 7 days in a month** |
| **Part-time** | Fewer than **35 hours a week** |

A new employee in regular employment can be placed on **probation** for up to
**six months**.

## Working hours and overtime

| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Normal working time | **8 hours a day**, **48 hours a week** |
| Break | **Half an hour** after **5 hours** of continuous work |
| Weekly holiday | **One day** a week |
| Overtime | Paid at **1.5 times** the normal wage |
| Overtime limit | **4 hours a day** and **24 hours a week** |

## Leave

| Leave | Entitlement |
| --- | --- |
| **Public holidays** | **13 days** a year, with **one extra day for women** |
| **Home leave** | **1 day** for every **20 days** worked |
| **Sick leave** | **12 days** a year, with **full pay** |
| **Maternity leave** | **98 days** in total, of which **60 days** are **paid** |
| **Paternity leave** | **15 days**, with pay |
| **Mourning leave** | **13 days**, with pay |

Some unused leave can be **accumulated**, within limits.

## Wages and benefits

- The **minimum wage** is set by the government on the recommendation of the
  **Minimum Wage Fixation Committee**, and **revised every two years**.
- Wages must be paid **at least monthly**, and on time.
- **Provident fund**: **10%** of basic salary from the employer, matched by
  the employee.
- **Gratuity**: **8.33%** of basic salary, paid by the employer.
- For workers covered by the **Social Security Fund**, the provident fund and
  gratuity become part of the **31%** contribution: **11%** from the employee
  and **20%** from the employer.
- **Bonuses** are governed by the separate **Bonus Act, 2030**.

## Occupational safety and health

- Employers must provide a **safe workplace**, **protective equipment** and
  **training**.
- Larger workplaces must form an **occupational safety and health committee**.
- **Sexual harassment** at work is prohibited, and employers must have a
  **complaint-handling** mechanism.

## Termination

- An employer can end employment for **misconduct**, **poor performance** or
  **genuine business reasons**, but must follow the **procedure**: notice, a
  chance to respond, and a fair decision.
- **Retrenchment**, meaning cutting jobs for business reasons, requires
  **advance notice** and **compensation**.
- An employee can **resign** by giving notice.
- On leaving, the employee must receive all **dues**: pay, leave encashment,
  gratuity and provident fund.

## Labour relations and disputes

- Workers can form **trade unions** and **bargain collectively**.
- **Individual disputes** go first to the employer, then to the **labour
  office** for mediation.
- **Collective disputes** go through **bargaining**, **mediation** and
  **arbitration**.
- The **Labour Court** hears labour cases and appeals.
- **Strikes and lockouts** are allowed only under the Act's conditions, with
  notice, and not in **essential services**.

## Relevance to banks

- Banks such as **RBB** have their own **employee service regulations**. These
  must give staff **at least the protections** the Labour Act provides.
- Bank HR policies on **leave, hours, overtime, harassment and grievances**
  have to follow the Act.

## Quick revision

> [!TIP]
> **One-line answers.** The **Labour Act, 2074** replaced the **2048** Act;
> its rules date from **2075**. Working time: **8 h a day / 48 h a week**.
> Overtime at **1.5×**, up to **4 h a day / 24 h a week**. Probation up to **6
> months**. Leave: sick **12 days**, maternity **98 days (60 paid)**, paternity
> **15 days**, mourning **13 days**, home leave **1 day per 20 days** worked.
> Minimum wage revised every **2 years**. PF **10% + 10%**; gratuity
> **8.33%**; SSF **31%**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Traps that show up in multiple-choice questions: **casual** work means **up
> to 7 days a month**; **part-time** means **under 35 hours a week**; only
> **60 of the 98** maternity days are **paid**; employment of children **under
> 14** is prohibited; **overtime** is paid at **1.5 times**, not double.

> [!WARNING]
> I have not checked these figures against the text of the Act. Leave days and
> wage rules in particular are worth checking against the **Labour Act, 2074**
> and **Labour Rules, 2075** on the Nepal Law Commission website.
