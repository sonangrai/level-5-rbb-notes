This note covers syllabus items **2.9 Know Your Customer** and **2.10 Audit
and Internal Control**. Both are part of how a bank **protects itself and the
financial system**:

- **KYC** keeps out **criminals and their money**.
- **Internal control and audit** catch **mistakes, fraud and weak management**
  inside the bank.

## Know Your Customer (KYC)

**KYC** is the process of **identifying and verifying customers** and
**understanding what they do**, before opening an account and for as long as
the relationship lasts. Its purpose is to stop banks being used for **money
laundering** and **terrorist financing**.

### Legal and regulatory framework

| Instrument | Role |
| --- | --- |
| **Assets (Money) Laundering Prevention Act, 2064 (2008)** | Main law; sets KYC and reporting duties for banks and other "reporting entities" |
| **NRB AML/CFT Directive** (in the Unified Directives) | Detailed KYC, due-diligence and reporting rules for BFIs |
| **Financial Information Unit (FIU-Nepal)** | Set up within **NRB**; receives and analyses reports from banks |
| **FATF / Asia-Pacific Group (APG)** | International standards; Nepal is a member of the **APG** |

> [!WARNING]
> In **February 2025** the Financial Action Task Force (**FATF**) placed Nepal
> on its **"grey list"** (jurisdictions under increased monitoring) because of
> weaknesses in its AML/CFT system. This makes KYC compliance especially
> important for Nepali banks.

### Elements of KYC

1. **Customer acceptance policy**: who the bank will and will not take as a
   customer. For example, it **refuses anonymous or fictitious-name
   accounts**.
2. **Customer identification**: collecting and **verifying** identity
   documents, such as a citizenship certificate, passport or national ID, plus
   a photograph and address. For companies: registration documents, PAN, and
   details of directors and **beneficial owners**.
3. **Risk categorisation**: rating each customer **low, medium or high risk**.
4. **Ongoing monitoring**: watching transactions for anything **unusual for
   that customer**, and **updating KYC** regularly. High-risk customers are
   updated more often.

### Customer due diligence (CDD)

| Level | Applied to |
| --- | --- |
| **Simplified CDD** | **Low-risk** customers, such as small savings accounts |
| **Normal CDD** | Most customers |
| **Enhanced CDD (EDD)** | **High-risk** customers: **politically exposed persons (PEPs)**, customers from high-risk countries, complex ownership structures, large cash businesses |

A **beneficial owner** is the **real person who ultimately owns or controls**
an account or company, even if it is held in someone else's name. Banks must
identify the beneficial owner, not just the name on the account.

### Reporting to FIU-Nepal

| Report | When |
| --- | --- |
| **Threshold Transaction Report (TTR)** | **Cash** transactions of **Rs 10 lakh (1 million) or more** in a single transaction or in a day |
| **Suspicious Transaction Report (STR)** | **Any** transaction the bank **suspects** is linked to crime, **whatever the amount** |

- **Tipping off** is an offence. The bank must **not tell the customer** that
  an STR has been filed.
- Banks must **keep records** of transactions and KYC documents for at least
  **5 years**.
- Each bank appoints a senior **AML/CFT compliance officer**.

## Audit and internal control

### Internal control

**Internal control** is the set of **policies, procedures and checks** a bank
uses to make sure that:

- its **operations are efficient and effective**;
- its **financial reports are reliable**;
- it **complies** with laws and regulations;
- its **assets are protected** from loss and fraud.

The widely used **COSO framework** describes **five components**:

| Component | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Control environment** | The "tone at the top": the board's and management's integrity, ethics and governance |
| **Risk assessment** | Identifying and analysing the risks to the bank's objectives |
| **Control activities** | The actual checks, such as approvals, verifications and reconciliations |
| **Information and communication** | Getting the right information to the right people on time |
| **Monitoring** | Continuously checking that the controls still work, through internal audit and reviews |

Common control activities in banks:

- **Segregation of duties**: one person never handles a transaction from
  start to finish.
- **Maker–checker** (dual control): one person enters a transaction and
  another approves it.
- **Joint custody** of cash, vault keys, security stationery and passwords.
- **Limits of authority**: lending and approval powers at each level are
  written down.
- **Job rotation** and **mandatory leave**, so that hidden fraud comes to
  light.
- **Daily reconciliation** of cash, accounts and inter-branch transactions.

### Three lines of defence

| Line | Who | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **First** | Business units and branches | Own and manage risk in daily operations |
| **Second** | Risk management and compliance | Set policies and **monitor** the first line |
| **Third** | **Internal audit** | **Independent assurance** to the board that the first two lines work |

### Internal audit

- An **independent department** inside the bank that checks branches,
  departments and systems.
- It reports to the board's **Audit Committee**, not to management, so that it
  stays independent.
- It works on a **risk-based audit plan**. Riskier areas are audited more
  often.
- Some banks also run a **concurrent audit**, which checks transactions as
  they happen.

### Audit Committee

Under **BAFIA** and NRB's **corporate governance** directive, every BFI's
board must form an **Audit Committee**:

- It is chaired by a **non-executive director**.
- The **head of internal audit** acts as its **secretary**.
- It reviews **internal audit findings**, **financial statements** and the
  **internal control system**, and recommends the **external auditor**.

### External (statutory) audit

- An **independent chartered accountant** audits the bank's annual financial
  statements and gives an **opinion** on whether they present a **true and
  fair view**.
- The **annual general meeting (AGM)** appoints the auditor, who must meet
  **NRB's eligibility conditions**. The same auditor cannot audit the bank
  for more than a set number of years in a row.
- The auditor also prepares a **Long Form Audit Report (LFAR)** for **NRB**.
  It reports in detail on the bank's internal control, loans, compliance and
  risks.
- The bank's audited financial statements need **NRB approval** before it can
  publish them and declare a **dividend**.

### NRB supervision

NRB also checks BFIs from outside:

- **On-site inspection**: NRB examiners visit the bank, usually through
  **risk-based supervision**.
- **Off-site supervision**: NRB monitors the bank continuously through the
  **regular reports** the bank submits.
- NRB can give **directives**, impose **penalties**, or in serious cases
  **take over management** of a BFI.

## Quick revision

> [!TIP]
> **One-line answers.** KYC law: **Assets (Money) Laundering Prevention Act,
> 2064**. **FIU-Nepal** sits within **NRB**. **TTR**: cash of **Rs 10 lakh or
> more**; **STR**: any suspicious transaction, **any amount**; keep records
> for **5 years**. **EDD** for **PEPs** and other high-risk customers. Nepal
> was **FATF grey-listed in February 2025**. **COSO** has five components;
> there are **three lines of defence**; internal audit reports to the
> **Audit Committee**; the external auditor sends an **LFAR** to NRB.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Traps that show up in multiple-choice questions: an **STR has no minimum
> amount**; **tipping off** a customer is an offence; **internal audit** is
> the **third** line of defence, not the second; the Audit Committee is
> chaired by a **non-executive** director; the **AGM** appoints the external
> auditor, not the CEO; **beneficial owner** means the real controlling
> person, not the account holder on paper.
