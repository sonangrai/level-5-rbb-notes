This note covers syllabus items **3.3 Payment and Settlement Act, 2075** and
**3.7 Electronic Transaction Act, 2063**. Together they make digital banking
legal and safe:

- The **Payment and Settlement Act** governs the **systems that move money**.
- The **Electronic Transaction Act** gives **electronic records and
  signatures** legal force and punishes **computer crime**.

## Payment and Settlement Act, 2075

The **Payment and Settlement Act, 2075 (2019)** gives **NRB** the legal power
to **regulate, license and oversee** Nepal's payment systems. Before it,
fast-growing services such as mobile wallets, card networks and clearing
houses had no dedicated law.

### Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Payment system** | The set of rules, instruments and technology used to move money between parties |
| **Payment System Operator (PSO)** | Runs a payment **system**, such as a clearing house, card switch or national payment switch |
| **Payment Service Provider (PSP)** | Provides payment **services** to users, such as a mobile wallet or other e-money service |
| **Clearing** | Exchanging and confirming payment instructions, and working out who owes whom |
| **Settlement** | The final transfer of money that ends the obligation |
| **Netting** | Offsetting what participants owe each other, so that only the **net** amount is settled |

### Main provisions

- **Licensing.** No one may run a payment system or provide payment services
  **without NRB's licence**.
- **Regulation and oversight.** NRB issues rules on **capital, security,
  interoperability, consumer protection and reporting**, and can **inspect**
  PSOs and PSPs.
- **Settlement finality.** Once a payment has been **settled**, it is **final
  and cannot be reversed**, even if a participant later becomes **insolvent**.
  This protects the whole system from a chain of failures.
- **Netting** arrangements are **legally valid**.
- **Electronic fund transfers** and **e-money** are legally recognised.
- **Customer protection.** Providers must keep customer funds secure and
  handle **complaints**.
- **Penalties** apply for operating without a licence, misusing customer funds
  and breaking NRB's directives.

NRB has filled in the detail through the **Payment and Settlement Bylaw** and a
**payment systems oversight framework**.

### Nepal's payment infrastructure

| System | Operator | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **RTGS** (Real-Time Gross Settlement) | **NRB** | High-value payments, settled **one by one in real time** |
| **ECC** (Electronic Cheque Clearing) | **NCHL** | Clearing of cheques from scanned images |
| **connectIPS / NCHL-IPS** | **NCHL** | Interbank and account-to-account fund transfers |
| **National Payment Switch (NPS)** | **NCHL** | Domestic switch for cards and QR, which cuts dependence on foreign card networks |
| **Wallets and QR networks** | Licensed PSPs and PSOs | Retail payments, such as eSewa, Khalti and Fonepay |

**NCHL** stands for **Nepal Clearing House Ltd.**

## Electronic Transaction Act, 2063

The **Electronic Transaction Act, 2063 (2008)** is Nepal's main law on
**electronic commerce** and **cyber crime**. The **Electronic Transaction
Rules, 2064** support it.

### Purpose

- To give **legal recognition** to **electronic records** and **digital
  signatures**, so that they count like paper documents and handwritten
  signatures.
- To make **electronic transactions** such as online banking and e-commerce
  **reliable and secure**.
- To **prevent and punish** crimes involving computers.

### Main provisions

| Area | Provision |
| --- | --- |
| **Electronic records** | Legally valid; may be kept in place of paper records if they are accessible and unaltered |
| **Digital signatures** | Legally recognised, using **asymmetric (public-key) cryptography** |
| **Controller of Certification** | Government office that **licenses and supervises Certifying Authorities** |
| **Certifying Authority (CA)** | Issues **digital signature certificates** |
| **Duties of subscribers** | Keep the **private key** secure and report if it is compromised |
| **Network service providers** | Not liable for third-party content they only carry, if they act in good faith |
| **IT Tribunal** | A specialised tribunal provided for to hear cases under the Act |

### Computer offences

The Act makes these **offences**, punishable by **fine, imprisonment or
both**:

- **Unauthorised access** to a computer or system (hacking).
- **Damaging** a computer system, or **destroying or changing data**.
- **Tampering** with a computer's **source code**.
- **Computer fraud**: using a computer to deceive and cause loss.
- **Publishing illegal material** in electronic form. This is the
  controversial **Section 47**, which has been used against online speech.
- **Breaching confidentiality** of electronic records.
- Making **false statements** to the Controller or a Certifying Authority, or
  **misusing** digital signature certificates.

### Relevance to banks

- **E-banking records** and **digitally signed documents** are valid
  evidence.
- **Hacking, card skimming, phishing and data theft** against banks can be
  prosecuted under this Act, alongside the **Banking Offence and Punishment
  Act**.
- Banks are expected to protect **customer data** and **system integrity**.

## Quick revision

> [!TIP]
> **One-line answers.** **Payment and Settlement Act, 2075 (2019)**: NRB
> licenses and oversees **PSOs** (systems) and **PSPs** (services); provides
> **settlement finality**; makes **netting** legally valid. **RTGS** is run
> by **NRB**; **ECC, connectIPS and NPS** by **NCHL**. **Electronic
> Transaction Act, 2063 (2008)**: legally recognises **electronic records and
> digital signatures**; the **Controller of Certification** licenses
> **Certifying Authorities**; defines **computer offences**; controversial
> **Section 47**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Traps that show up in multiple-choice questions: a **PSO** runs a
> **system** while a **PSP** offers a **service**; **settlement finality**
> means a settled payment **cannot be reversed**, even if the participant
> becomes insolvent; **RTGS** settles **gross**, one payment at a time, not
> netted; digital signatures use **public-key** cryptography; the
> **Electronic Transaction Act (2063)** is **older** than the **Payment and
> Settlement Act (2075)**.
