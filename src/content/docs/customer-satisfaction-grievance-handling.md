This note covers syllabus items **4.5 Customer Satisfaction and Delivery** and
**4.6 Grievance Handling**. Both are about keeping people **satisfied and
loyal**:

- **Customers**, through good service, and fair handling when something goes
  wrong.
- **Employees**, through a fair system for raising problems at work.

## Customer satisfaction

**Customer satisfaction** is how far a product or service **meets or exceeds
what the customer expected**:

> **Satisfaction = Perceived performance − Expectations**

- If performance falls **below** expectations, the customer is
  **dissatisfied**.
- If it **meets** them, the customer is **satisfied**.
- If it **exceeds** them, the customer is **delighted** and likely to stay
  loyal.

### Why it matters for a bank

- Banks sell **similar products**, so **service** is what makes a bank stand
  out.
- **Keeping** a customer costs much less than **winning a new one**.
- **Satisfied customers** use **more products**, such as loans, cards and
  insurance, and **recommend** the bank to others.
- **Dissatisfied customers** leave, complain publicly and damage the bank's
  **reputation**.

## Service quality: the SERVQUAL model

**SERVQUAL**, developed by **Parasuraman, Zeithaml and Berry**, measures
service quality as the **gap between expectations and perceptions** on **five
dimensions**. They are easy to remember as **RATER**:

| Dimension | Meaning | Example in a bank |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **R**eliability | Delivering what was promised, **accurately and dependably** | Correct statements, transfers completed on time |
| **A**ssurance | Staff **knowledge and courtesy** that build **trust** | Staff who can explain loan terms clearly |
| **T**angibles | Physical appearance: premises, equipment, staff | A clean branch, working ATMs, a user-friendly app |
| **E**mpathy | **Individual care** and attention | Helping elderly customers, understanding needs |
| **R**esponsiveness | **Willingness** to help and **prompt** service | Short queues, quick replies to complaints |

Of the five, **reliability** is usually the **most important** to customers.

## Service delivery

**Service delivery** is how the bank actually provides its services to
customers.

### Delivery channels

- **Branches** and **extension counters**.
- **ATMs** and **cash deposit machines**.
- **Mobile banking**, **internet banking** and **QR payments**.
- **Branchless banking** and **agents** in remote areas.
- **Call centres** and **social-media support**.

### Good practices

- A **Citizen Charter** (*Nagarik Badapatra*): a public display of the
  bank's **services, required documents, fees and time taken**.
- **Service standards**, such as a maximum waiting time or same-day account
  opening.
- **Queue management** and **help desks**.
- **Transparency** about **interest rates, fees and charges**.
- **Staff training** in products, soft skills and customer handling.
- **Measuring satisfaction** through **surveys**, **mystery shopping**,
  **complaint analysis** and the **Net Promoter Score (NPS)**.

### Moments of truth

A **moment of truth** is any point where a customer **comes into contact**
with the bank: a phone call, a visit, an app login. Each one shapes how the
customer sees the bank. One bad experience can undo many good ones.

## Customer grievance handling

A **grievance** (*gunaso*) is a **complaint** or feeling of **unfair
treatment**. In banking, customers may complain about **wrong charges, failed
transactions, delays, staff behaviour, card or ATM problems, or loan terms**.

### NRB's consumer-protection framework

NRB requires BFIs to protect **financial consumers**. Its **Unified
Directives** on **consumer protection and grievance handling** require each
BFI to:

- appoint a **grievance handling officer**, and display their **name and
  contact details** at every office and on the website;
- set up **complaint channels**: counter, phone, email, website and app;
- **acknowledge** each complaint and **resolve it within a set time**;
- **record** complaints and **report** them to NRB regularly;
- give customers clear information on **interest rates, fees and terms**, and
  not charge undisclosed fees.

If a BFI **does not resolve** a complaint, the customer can take it to
**NRB**, which runs an **online grievance portal**.

### Steps in handling a customer complaint

1. **Listen** fully, without interrupting or arguing.
2. **Acknowledge and apologise** for the inconvenience. This is not the same
   as admitting fault.
3. **Record** the complaint and give a **reference number**.
4. **Investigate** the facts.
5. **Resolve** the problem, or explain clearly if no change is possible.
6. **Follow up** to confirm the customer is satisfied.
7. **Analyse** complaints for patterns, and fix the **root cause**.

> [!NOTE]
> A complaint that is **handled well** can make a customer **more loyal** than
> before. This is called the **service recovery paradox**.

## Employee grievance handling

An **employee grievance** is **dissatisfaction** that an employee raises about
their **job or treatment at work**. Common causes:

- **Pay**, allowances or **promotion** decisions.
- **Transfers**, workload or working conditions.
- **Unfair treatment**, discrimination or harassment.
- Disputes over **leave**, **discipline** or performance ratings.
- **Poor relations** with a supervisor.

### A grievance procedure

A good organisation has a **written, step-by-step procedure**:

1. **Immediate supervisor**: most grievances should be settled here,
   informally and quickly.
2. **Department or branch head**, if the first step fails.
3. **HR department** or a **grievance committee**.
4. **Top management**, or an **appeal**.
5. **Outside bodies**, such as a labour office or court, under labour law, if
   the matter is still unresolved.

### Principles

- **Open door policy**: employees feel free to raise problems.
- **Time limits** for each step.
- **Confidentiality** and **no retaliation** against the employee.
- **Fairness**: hear both sides, and decide based on facts and policy.
- **Records** of grievances and decisions.
- **Root-cause correction**, so the same grievance does not keep coming
  back.

### Why it matters

- Problems are settled **early**, before they turn into **disputes, strikes or
  resignations**.
- **Morale, trust and productivity** improve.
- Management learns about **policy problems**.

## Quick revision

> [!TIP]
> **One-line answers.** Satisfaction = **perceived performance −
> expectations**. **SERVQUAL / RATER**: **R**eliability, **A**ssurance,
> **T**angibles, **E**mpathy, **R**esponsiveness; **reliability** matters
> most. A **Citizen Charter** displays services, documents, fees and time.
> NRB requires a **grievance handling officer** in every BFI, with unresolved
> complaints going to **NRB**. Employee grievances should be handled first by
> the **immediate supervisor**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Traps that show up in multiple-choice questions: **exceeding** expectations
> creates **delight**; **tangibles** means physical facilities, not the
> product; the **service recovery paradox** means a well-handled complaint can
> **increase** loyalty; an employee grievance procedure must protect against
> **retaliation**; **keeping** a customer is **cheaper** than winning a new
> one.
