The **National Information and Communication Technology Policy, 2015
(2072 BS)** is the Government of Nepal's overarching policy for developing
and using ICT across every sector of society and the economy. It was issued
by the **Ministry of Information and Communication**, Singhadurbar,
Kathmandu, and lays the "foundational groundwork for an overarching vision of
**Digital Nepal**". Most of its numeric targets were set for **2020**.

> [!NOTE]
> This summary follows the policy document published by the Ministry of
> Information and Communication. Section numbers below match that document.

## Background: past policy endeavours

The ICT Policy 2015 builds on a series of earlier policies and laws (section
2 of the policy):

| Year | Instrument | Contribution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1992 | **National Communication Policy** | Paved the way for liberalising the telecommunication sector |
| 1997 | **Telecommunications Act and Regulations** | Created the legal and institutional framework for regulating the sector (and the Nepal Telecommunications Authority) |
| 2000 | **Information Technology (IT) Policy** | First policy to position IT as a tool for development and growth |
| 2004 | **Telecommunications Policy** | Intensified liberalisation — technology neutrality and an open licensing regime |
| 2008 (2063 BS) | **Electronic Transactions Act** | Legal recognition of electronic records and digital signatures; defines computer crimes |
| 2010 | **IT Policy revised** | Updated the 2000 IT Policy |
| 2014 | **Draft Broadband Policy** | Aimed at a favourable environment for ICT growth |
| **2015** | **National ICT Policy** | Brings telecom, IT and broadcasting under one converged ICT policy |

Results recognised by the policy include rapid growth in mobile telephony and
internet uptake brought about by a liberalised, competitive telecom sector.

## Vision and mission

| | Statement |
| --- | --- |
| **Vision** (section 8) | "To transform Nepal into an **information and knowledge-based society and economy**." |
| **Mission** (section 9) | "To create conditions for the intensified development and growth of ICT sector as a **key driver for Nepal's sustainable development and poverty reduction** strategies." |

## Guiding principles (section 5)

1. Create the groundwork for an overarching vision of **"Digital Nepal"**,
   with ICT as a key driving force in transforming Nepali society into a
   knowledge and information based society.
2. Implementation will be **government-led and private-sector-driven**, with
   **public–private partnerships (PPP)** as one basis, especially for projects
   of national importance; the government leads in mobilising investment for
   national telecom backbone and broadband infrastructure.
3. **Mainstream ICT in all sectors** of the economy, within the national
   socio-economic development and poverty reduction agenda.
4. **Link** policy goals with national development objectives such as the
   **periodic plans of the National Planning Commission**; all sectors draw
   their ICT strategies from this policy.
5. Promote **infrastructural synergies** — roll out telecom and broadband along
   with roads and electricity.
6. Support the policy with legislation that **separates policy-making,
   regulation and operation**, in line with technological convergence.
7. Align with regional and global strategies — **UN MDGs, WSIS beyond 2015,
   WTO, the Broadband Commission, UN-ESCAP** and the post-2015 sustainable
   development agenda.
8. Uphold **freedom of expression on the internet** and **net neutrality**.
9. Promote **platform-neutral services** in e-governance.

## Goals and targets (section 6)

| No. | Goal / target | Deadline |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6.1 | Enhance national ICT readiness — be at least in the **top second quartile** of the international **ICT Development Index** and **e-Government rankings** | 2020 |
| 6.2 | At least **75 %** of the population will have **digital literacy** skills | End of 2020 |
| 6.3 | Universal broadband access on an equitable basis — **90 %** of the population able to access **broadband** | 2020 |
| 6.4 | ICT value added (including digital content and services) to account for at least **7.5 % of GDP** | 2020 |
| 6.5 | Restructure the apex institution: rename the ministry as the **Ministry of Information Technology and Communication**, the focal ministry for all ICT matters (O&M study to restructure within 2016) | 2016 |
| 6.6 | Expand the jurisdiction of the **Nepal Telecommunications Authority** and rename it the **Nepal Communication Authority** | — |
| 6.7 | **Entire population** to have access to the **internet** | 2020 |
| 6.8 | **80 %** of all citizen-facing government services offered **online** | 2020 |
| 6.9 | Promote **e-Procurement** covering **all public procurement**, irrespective of cost and volume | 2018 |
| 6.10 | G2G implementation for **complete automation** of land administration, revenue administration, vital registration, passport and citizenship certificate services | 2020 |
| 6.11 | Broadband internet user penetration of **30 %** at a minimum of **512 kbps**, and at least **10 Mbps** download speed on demand in **urban** areas | 2018 |
| 6.12 | Special funding arrangements to support **innovation and incubation** of online businesses and start-ups | Within 2018 |

> [!TIP]
> **Numbers most often asked:** digital literacy **75 %**, broadband access
> **90 %**, ICT share of GDP **7.5 %**, online government services **80 %**,
> broadband speed **512 kbps** minimum and **10 Mbps** in urban areas — mostly
> by **2020**.

## Objectives (section 11, selected)

- Empower Nepal's participation in the **global knowledge society**.
- Transform **government service delivery** — transparency, efficiency,
  inclusiveness and participation through ICT.
- Use ICT to raise **productivity** in key economic sectors.
- Build an efficient, interoperable, **secure**, reliable and sustainable
  national ICT infrastructure that meets international standards.
- Ensure universal access, **quality of service** and cost-effective
  connectivity for internet, e-commerce and e-government.
- Provide affordable **last-mile** access and public access points, especially
  in **rural and underserved** areas.
- Develop **local and indigenous content** and Nepal's linguistic and cultural
  heritage online.
- Promote participation of **youth and women**; address gender inequalities.
- Promote **free and open source software** and open standards in government.
- Create an innovative, competitive and well-regulated **ICT industry**.

## Cyber security provisions

### Policy (section 7.21 — Building confidence and security in the use of ICTs)

1. Develop and implement **cyber security policies** that enhance individual
   and collective security while preserving citizens' **right to privacy** and
   other fundamental freedoms.
2. **Mandate** organisations providing public information and communication
   services (telecom, internet, email) to adopt administrative and
   technological measures enabling **national security agencies** to curb misuse
   and unsolicited content.
3. Secure full implementation of the **Electronic Transactions Act**, with
   amendments to allow effective **investigation and prosecution of cyber
   crime**.

### Strategies (section 12.21)

| Strategy | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Legal framework and enforcement | A national initiative to maintain an effective legal framework and capability to disrupt and prosecute cyber crime; make it easier to **report** cyber crime |
| Awareness | Programmes to educate and empower people and firms to protect themselves online |
| Government capacity | A government-wide capacity-building initiative to deter and defend against high-end threats |
| **National cyber security cell** | Established within government to prevent, detect, defend against and recover from cyber attacks |
| Education | Strengthen **postgraduate** cyber security education with academia; explore cyber security education at all levels |
| Certified training | Certified specialist training programmes with the private sector |
| ISPs | Encourage ISPs to help users find out whether their computers are compromised and how to fix them |
| **NCERT** | Establish a government-wide **National Computer Emergency Response Team** to respond to cyber security issues |
| Police | Significantly increase law enforcement capability on cyber crime **by 2018**; police to **mainstream cyber awareness from 2016** |
| Professional certification | A scheme certifying the competence of information assurance and cyber security professionals **by December 2016**, and certifying specialist training **in 2018** |
| Ethical hackers | Encourage a community of **ethical hackers** to help protect national networks |
| **Computer Crimes Investigation Unit** and **National Electronic Communication Security Centre** | Established within appropriate agencies to enforce the Electronic Transactions Act and protect the availability, authenticity, integrity and confidentiality of networks, data and consumer privacy |

## Institutional arrangements (section 13)

### ICT Policy Implementation Steering Committee

A **National ICT Policy Implementation Steering Committee** is formed at the
ministry responsible for ICT:

| Position | Role |
| --- | --- |
| **Hon. Minister**, Ministry of Information and Communication (Technology) | **Chairperson** |
| Member, **National Planning Commission** | Member |
| Secretaries of the Ministries of **Education, Health, Agriculture, Finance, Local Development**, and **Information and Communication** | Members |
| Chairman, **Nepal Telecommunications Authority** | Member |
| Private sector / domain experts | **2** members |
| **Joint Secretary** (Policy and Programme), Ministry of Information and Communication | **Member-Secretary** |

Its main role is **overall coordination** of policy implementation and
**monitoring and evaluation**. It forms an **ICT Policy Implementation
Sub-Committee** of stakeholders and domain experts, including the private
sector, for expert advice.

## Later developments

The ICT Policy 2015 set the direction for several later initiatives:

- The ministry was restructured as the **Ministry of Communication and
  Information Technology (MoCIT)**.
- **Digital Nepal Framework (2019)** — a blueprint of digital initiatives across
  eight sectors, building on the policy's "Digital Nepal" vision.
- **Nepal Telecommunications Authority** continues as the regulator.
- A dedicated **National Cyber Security Policy (2023 / 2080 BS)** and the
  **National Cyber Security Centre** carry forward the security provisions of
  section 7.21.
