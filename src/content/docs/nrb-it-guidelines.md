**Nepal Rastra Bank (NRB)**, the central bank of Nepal, issued the
**Information Technology Guidelines** in **August 2012** through its **Bank
Supervision Department**. They set the minimum standards that banks must
follow to govern IT, secure information, run IT operations, manage
outsourcing, plan for business continuity, audit their systems and fight
electronic fraud.

## Why NRB issued the guidelines

The executive summary explains the background:

- IT has become **part of the business** of banks rather than just a support
  function, creating challenges of managing and governing it.
- New **delivery channels** — ATM, internet banking, mobile banking — have
  increased the risk of **financial loss and electronic fraud**.
- Technology risk affects not only **operational risk** but also credit,
  reputation, compliance, market and strategic risk.
- **Virtualisation, outsourced data centres and DR sites, and security
  outsourcing** bring new challenges.
- **Cyber fraud** by insiders and outsiders — card, ATM, internet and mobile
  banking fraud — is growing.

**Objectives:** to strengthen banks to **tackle emerging cyber frauds**,
**manage IT prudently**, and **mitigate risks** from implementing IT — and to
promote **sound and robust technology risk management** and strengthen
**system security, reliability, availability and business continuity**.

## Applicability and compliance

| Item | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Applies to | Banks — defined as **banks and financial institutions licensed by NRB** (the guidelines were addressed primarily to commercial banks) |
| Full compliance | **Within two years** from the date of issue |
| Action plan | An action plan, with a time frame for each action, submitted to the **Bank Supervision Department within six months** of issue |
| Monitoring | Compliance examined during NRB's periodic **on-site and off-site supervision** |

## 1. IT governance

1. A **board-approved IT strategy and IT policy**; the IT policy reviewed **at
   least annually**. Long-term strategy mapped to short-term strategy; detailed
   operational procedures for all IT operations.
2. IT **organisational structure** proportionate to the bank's size and
   business — broadly **Development, Technology, IT Operations and Information
   Assurance**.
3. Assess required **expertise** and periodic **training** needs of IT staff.
4. A system to **monitor and measure IT performance**, reported to management.
5. **IT risk** included in the risk management or operational risk policy,
   covering e-banking and supplier activities.
6. Banks are encouraged to adopt an international IT control framework such as
   **COBIT**.
7. The **board** should be aware of the bank's IT resources and ensure they are
   sufficient.
8. Designate a **senior official as Information Security Officer (ISO)** —
   responsible for **enforcing the information security policy** and
   coordinating security issues internally and externally.
9. **Detailed risk analysis before adopting new technology**, as part of the
   product approval process, covering security, regulatory and legal aspects
   and testing.
10. Identify and address **legal risk** arising from cyber law and the
    **Electronic Transactions Act** and related laws.

## 2. Information security

The guidelines list 30 requirements. The key ones:

| Area | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Policy | A **board-approved Information Security Policy** covering all electronic delivery channels and payment systems, communicated to staff, contractors and consultants |
| Risk assessment | **At least annually**, for each asset that could affect the **CIA** of information |
| Access | On a **"need to know"** basis with **least privilege**, for the required time only; **privileged users** closely supervised and logged |
| Physical security | Physical and environmental controls based on location, building and neighbourhood |
| Testing | Regularly assess security health; **periodic penetration testing** recommended |
| Hardening | Highest security settings on OS, firewalls and system software; **change default passwords** immediately; install security patches |
| Malware | Comprehensive **virus protection** |
| Encryption | **Strong cryptography and end-to-end encryption** for PINs, passwords and sensitive data, in transit and storage |
| Firewalls | Between **internal and external networks** and between geographically separate sites |
| Customer authentication | Verify customers before granting access and before executing transactions |
| Audit trail | Detailed audit trail — **transaction ID, date, time, originator ID, authoriser ID, action** — secured and retained even after system migration |
| Data integrity | Applications free of malware and hidden channels; integrity of databases, warehouses and archives; **never update the database directly at the back end** except under close supervision and authorisation |
| Data abroad | If Nepal data is stored or processed abroad: segregation, strict access control, change control, and **unimpeded NRB access and right to inspect** |
| Migration | A **migration policy**; sign-off by application owners at each stage |
| Classification | Information assets recorded and **classified by criticality**, with matching controls |
| Staff screening | **Background and credit checks** for privileged staff (system administrators, security officers) |
| Data security | Covers disposal, portable devices, media in transit and storage, encryption of customer data |
| Wireless | Additional controls for wireless networks |
| **ATM** | **CCTV** at every ATM with adequate lighting — but must **not capture the PIN**; encrypted transmission; secure key and PIN management; segregation of duties in card and PIN creation |
| **Card and PIN** | Card and PIN never under the control of **one person**, and **never together** before reaching the customer |
| **POS** | Replace signature-based POS with **PIN-based** authorisation |
| **Chip cards** | Replace **magnetic stripe** cards with **chip-based** cards |
| **Online card payments** | **Second-factor authentication** and **instant alerts** (email / SMS / voice) |
| **Mobile banking** | Encrypt transactions and PINs; **daily and per-transaction limits**; mobile banking only for **Nepalese currency** accounts |
| **Internet banking** | **More than one factor** of authentication for critical activities such as fund transfers |
| Web applications | Protect against cyber attacks; critical applications use the latest **SSL** encryption |

## 3. Information security education

- Develop an **information security awareness programme** for **employees,
  vendors, customers** and other stakeholders, tailored to each group; track
  its effectiveness.
- Educate **customers** to secure their own devices and credentials, and
  respond promptly to their security queries.
- Banks remain responsible for the safety of their systems and must
  authenticate customers properly.

## 4. Information disclosure and grievance handling

- Publish the **dispute resolution process** for security breaches and fraud,
  and when losses fall on the bank or the customer.
- Publish the **privacy and security policy** and transaction costs on the
  website or at subscription.
- Inform users of **ATM transaction costs** before the transaction.
- A **dispute-handling mechanism** with expected response times; the bank is
  responsible for **grievance handling**.
- Explain the **risks and benefits** of each e-banking channel and what
  protection it does and does not provide.

## 5. Outsourcing management

1. The **board and senior management** are accountable for outsourcing
   decisions and risks.
2. **Evaluate risk** before outsourcing, and periodically.
3. Outsourced operations are subject to the bank's **security and privacy
   policy**.
4. Outsourcing must not obstruct regulators; agreements must recognise
   **NRB's authority** under **BAFIA** and the **NRB Act** to inspect the
   service provider.
5. Monitor providers through **service level agreements** and periodic reviews
   of their financial and operational condition.
6. The bank remains responsible for CIA even if the provider sub-outsources.
7. Contingency plans for **alternate providers** or bringing services back
   **in-house**.
8. For **offshore outsourcing**, consider **country risk** and have an **exit
   strategy**.
9. Obtain **source code** or a **software escrow** agreement for critical
   applications in case the vendor goes out of business.
10. For cloud, data-centre and DR hosting, clarify **data jurisdiction** and
    applicable regulations at the start.

## 6. IT operations

- Board and senior management oversee IT operations.
- **Segregation of duties**; documented procedures; access by **least
  privilege** and need to know.
- **Joint (dual) custody** for critical functions — system initialisation,
  network security configuration, firewall and IPS implementation, changing OS
  parameters, invoking emergency procedures, creating **master passwords and
  cryptographic keys**.
- A **change management** process — changes recorded, assessed, authorised,
  prioritised, planned, tested, implemented, documented and reviewed.
- A documented **migration policy**; audit trail of the old system retained.
- **Vendors and consultants** with access to critical systems are supervised
  like internal staff.
- **High availability** — standby hardware, software and network
  components.
- Periodic **risk assessment** of the IT environment — people, technology and
  processes.

## 7. Information systems acquisition, development and implementation

- Document and approve **functional, security and performance requirements**
  before development.
- Build **security into every stage of the SDLC** — access control,
  authentication, authorisation, logging, audit trail, data integrity.
- Audit trails detailed enough to serve as **forensic evidence**.
- Banks are encouraged to carry out **source code reviews**; fix all
  vulnerabilities before going live.

## 8. Business continuity and disaster recovery planning

The guidelines describe **BCP** as covering people, process and technology —
including business impact analysis, recovery strategies, testing, training,
awareness, communication and crisis management — and **DRP** as the
**technical part of BCP**.

1. A **board-approved BCP policy**, with procedures for prioritising critical
   functions and incident handling; reviewed periodically.
2. A **senior officer appointed Head of BCP**, responsible for developing,
   updating, testing and training.
3. BCP considers natural and man-made disasters, security threats, regulatory
   requirements, outsourcing dependencies, and the **people** aspect.
4. A **BCP team** of senior officers at **head office and branch offices**.
5. BCP **tested at least annually** — covering people, processes and
   technology; **both planned and unplanned** tests; **audited by internal
   audit**; results documented and the plan amended.
6. BCP specifies **RPO and RTO**; the bank may choose a **hot, warm or cold
   site** as long as it meets them.
7. Standby site may be the bank's own or **outsourced**; options include high
   availability, live replication, off-site backups.
8. Mitigation: local mirroring, **UPS and generator**, surge protectors, and
   physical and environmental controls in the data centre.
9. Data centre, DR, network, security and delivery channels designed for **high
   availability and no single point of failure**.
10. Data centre location chosen to minimise **natural and man-made** risks;
    physical access restricted.
11. **Check transaction and data integrity between DC and DR site**
    periodically — recommended as part of **End of Day (EOD)** or **Beginning
    of Day (BOD)**.
12. An **incident response plan** — detection, recovery of e-banking,
    communication with external parties and media, and alerting regulators.

See [Disaster recovery planning](/docs/disaster-recovery-planning) for the
underlying concepts.

## 9. IS audit

- Banks should conduct an **IS audit annually**.
- The **board or audit committee** provides resources for an audit team able to
  evaluate IT controls.
- IS audit may be **outsourced** to qualified external professionals, but the
  bank remains responsible for **audit planning, risk assessment and
  follow-up**; the audit committee ensures the provider's expertise.

## 10. Fraud management

1. Identify and document all **electronic attacks and suspected attacks** and
   **report them to NRB monthly**.
2. Make **customers aware** of frauds and how to identify, avoid and protect
   against them.

## Key concepts from the glossary and endnotes

| Concept | As explained in the guidelines |
| --- | --- |
| CIA | Confidentiality, integrity, availability — the basic principles of information security |
| Extra principles for banks | **Authenticity, non-repudiation, identification, authorisation, accountability and auditability** |
| Information classification (after ISO 17799) | **Top Secret, Highly Confidential, Proprietary, Internal Use, Public** |
| RPO | Amount of data, measured in time, that can be lost in a disaster |
| RTO | Time it takes to recover from a disaster |
| Cold site | No hardware, software or data backup — cheapest, slowest |
| Warm site | Hardware and connectivity in place; data backup may be a few days old |
| Hot site | Near duplicate with real-time synchronisation — most expensive; widely used by financial institutions |
| COBIT | An IT governance framework from the IT Governance Institute |
