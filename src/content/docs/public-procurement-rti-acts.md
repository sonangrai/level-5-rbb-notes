This note covers syllabus items **5.3 Public Procurement Act, 2063** and **5.4
Right to Information Act, 2064**. Both apply to **public bodies**. These
include **government-owned companies**, so they apply to **Rastriya Banijya
Bank**, which the government owns. Both laws aim at **transparency and
accountability** in how public bodies work.

## Public Procurement Act, 2063

The **Public Procurement Act, 2063 (2007)** governs how **public entities buy
goods, works and services**. It is supported by the **Public Procurement
Regulations, 2064**.

### Objectives

- **Competition**: give every qualified supplier a fair chance.
- **Transparency**: open, published procedures.
- **Fairness**: no favouritism or discrimination.
- **Value for money**: economy and efficiency.
- **Accountability**: decisions can be checked and challenged.

### Public Procurement Monitoring Office (PPMO)

The **PPMO** works under the **Office of the Prime Minister and Council of
Ministers**. It:

- sets **standard bidding documents** and guidelines;
- **monitors** procurement by public entities;
- runs the **electronic Government Procurement (e-GP)** system;
- **debars (blacklists)** bidders who commit fraud or corruption.

### Procurement methods

| Method | Used for |
| --- | --- |
| **Open competitive bidding** | The **default** method for large procurement. National or international; anyone qualified can bid |
| **Limited (restricted) bidding** | When only a few suppliers can meet the requirement |
| **Sealed quotation** | Smaller purchases, below a set value |
| **Direct procurement** | Very small purchases, or emergencies and single-source cases |
| **Request for proposals (RFP)** | **Consulting services**, chosen by quality and cost |
| **Users' committee** | Small local works carried out by the community |
| **Framework agreement** | Repeated purchases of common items over a period |

The value limits for each method are set in the **Regulations** and are
revised from time to time.

### Key rules

- **Procurement planning**: entities must prepare an **annual procurement
  plan**. Large projects also need a **master procurement plan**.
- **Cost estimate**: an estimate is made **before** inviting bids.
- **Splitting** a procurement into smaller parts to avoid open bidding is
  **prohibited**.
- **Bid security** and **performance security** are required.
- **e-bidding** is required for most public procurement, through the e-GP
  system.
- Bids are evaluated on **published criteria**. The contract usually goes to
  the **lowest evaluated substantially responsive bid**.
- **Complaints**: a bidder can complain to the **head of the public entity**
  within **7 days**. If not satisfied, they can go to the **Review
  Committee**.
- **Code of conduct**: officials and bidders must avoid **conflicts of
  interest**, **fraud** and **collusion**.

## Right to Information Act, 2064

The **Right to Information (RTI) Act, 2064 (2007)** puts into effect the
**right to information** guaranteed by the **Constitution (Article 27)**.
Every **Nepali citizen** has the right to **ask for and receive information**
held by **public bodies**.

### Public bodies covered

- Government offices.
- **Constitutional and statutory bodies**.
- **Public enterprises and companies the government owns or controls**,
  including **state-owned banks**.
- Political parties, and NGOs that receive public funds.

### National Information Commission (NIC)

- An **independent body** that **protects and promotes** the right to
  information.
- It has a **Chief Information Commissioner** and up to **two** Information
  Commissioners.
- It hears **appeals**, can **order** information to be released and can
  **penalise** officials.

### Process

```text
Citizen's request → Information Officer
    ↓ refused or no answer
Appeal to Head of the public body (within 7 days)
    ↓ still not satisfied
Appeal to National Information Commission (within 35 days)
```

| Step | Time limit |
| --- | --- |
| Information provided | **Immediately**, or within **15 days** |
| Information about a person's **life or safety** | Within **24 hours** |
| Appeal to the head of the body | Within **7 days**; the head decides within **7 days** |
| Appeal to the NIC | Within **35 days**; the NIC decides within **60 days** |

### Duties of public bodies

- Appoint an **Information Officer**.
- **Publish information proactively** every **three months**: structure,
  functions, staff, decisions, income and spending.
- **Keep records** properly so that information can be found.
- **Charge only the actual cost** of providing information.

### Information that can be withheld

- Information that would harm **national sovereignty, integrity or
  security**.
- Information that would obstruct the **investigation or prosecution** of a
  crime.
- Information that would seriously harm **economic, trade or monetary
  interests**, or **banking and commercial secrets**.
- Information that would harm **harmony between communities**.
- Information about a person's **privacy**, or that would endanger their life
  or property.

### Whistleblower protection

An employee who discloses **corruption or wrongdoing** in a public body in good
faith is **protected**. They cannot be **punished, dismissed or harassed** for
it.

### RTI in a bank

- **Customer account information** is protected by **banking
  confidentiality** under BAFIA, so it can be **withheld**.
- General information about the bank's **operations, decisions, procurement
  and spending** can usually be **requested**.
