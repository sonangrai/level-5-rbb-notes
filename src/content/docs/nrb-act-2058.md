This note covers syllabus item **3.1 Nepal Rastra Bank Act, 2058**.

**Nepal Rastra Bank (NRB)** is Nepal's **central bank**. It was established on
**14 Baisakh 2013 BS (26 April 1956)** under the **Nepal Rastra Bank Act,
2012**. The **Nepal Rastra Bank Act, 2058 (2002)** replaced that law. Its main
change was to make NRB **autonomous**, so that it can run monetary policy and
supervise banks **without day-to-day government control**. The Act has been
amended several times since, most notably in **2063** and **2073**.

## Objectives of NRB

The Act gives NRB these objectives:

1. To keep **prices stable** and the **balance of payments stable**, so that
   the economy can grow sustainably.
2. To keep the **banking and financial sector stable** and liquid.
3. To develop a **secure, healthy and efficient payment system**.
4. To **regulate, inspect, supervise and monitor** the banking and financial
   system.
5. To **expand access to financial services** across the country.

## Functions and powers

| Area | What NRB does |
| --- | --- |
| **Currency** | Has the **sole right to issue** Nepali banknotes and coins, and manages the currency in circulation |
| **Monetary policy** | Sets **monetary policy** every year and uses tools such as the policy rate, CRR, SLR and open market operations |
| **Foreign exchange** | Sets **foreign exchange policy**, manages the **exchange-rate system** and Nepal's **foreign exchange reserves**, and licenses money changers and remittance companies |
| **Banker to the government** | Is the government's **banker, financial adviser and fiscal agent**: keeps government accounts and manages **public debt** such as treasury bills and development bonds |
| **Banker to banks** | Holds BFIs' reserves, runs settlement, and is the **lender of last resort** |
| **Regulation and supervision** | **Licenses**, regulates, inspects and supervises BFIs, and issues the **Unified Directives** |
| **Payment systems** | Regulates and oversees payment systems, a role made stronger by the **Payment and Settlement Act, 2075** |
| **Problem BFIs** | Can **declare a BFI problematic**, take over its management, and order its **merger, sale or liquidation** |

## Governance

### Board of Directors

NRB is run by a **Board of Directors** of **seven** members:

| Member | Role |
| --- | --- |
| **Governor** | **Chairperson** |
| **Secretary, Ministry of Finance** | Ex-officio member |
| **Two Deputy Governors** | Members |
| **Three directors** | Experts in economics, banking, finance or law, appointed by the government |

### Governor

- The **Government of Nepal (Council of Ministers)** appoints the Governor on
  the recommendation of a **committee chaired by the Finance Minister**.
- The term is **five years**.
- The Act sets out **limited grounds for removal**, such as incapacity,
  misconduct or insolvency. This protects NRB's independence, because the
  government cannot remove a Governor simply for disagreeing with them.

## Autonomy and accountability

**Autonomy:**

- NRB makes **monetary and foreign exchange policy on its own**.
- **Lending to the government is limited**, so that the government cannot
  simply finance its deficit by having NRB print money.

**Accountability:**

- NRB publishes its **Monetary Policy** every year and reviews it during the
  year.
- It **reports to the government** on its activities and on the state of the
  economy.
- NRB's **accounts are audited** every year.

## Why the Act matters for banks

- It is the **source of NRB's power** to issue directives to BFIs and to punish
  them when they break the rules.
- It lets NRB act quickly when a **BFI gets into trouble**, to protect
  depositors.
- It works together with
  **[BAFIA, 2073](/docs/bafia-2073)**, which governs the BFIs themselves.

## Quick revision

> [!TIP]
> **One-line answers.** NRB was established on **14 Baisakh 2013 BS (1956)**
> under the **NRB Act, 2012**. The current law is the **NRB Act, 2058
> (2002)**, which made NRB **autonomous**. Objectives: **price and BoP
> stability**, **financial stability**, an **efficient payment system**,
> **supervision** and **financial access**. The board has **7 members**, with
> the **Governor as chair**. The Governor's term is **5 years**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Traps that show up in multiple-choice questions: the **2058** Act **replaced
> the 2012 Act**; it did **not** create NRB. The **Finance Secretary** sits on
> the board **ex officio**. NRB is the **lender of last resort**. It has the
> **sole right to issue currency**.
