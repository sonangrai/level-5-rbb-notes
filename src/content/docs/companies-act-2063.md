This note covers syllabus item **5.1 Companies Act, 2063**.

The **Companies Act, 2063 (2006)** governs how companies in Nepal are
**incorporated, managed, merged and wound up**. It replaced the **Companies
Act, 2053**, and has been amended since, notably in **2074**.

The **Office of the Company Registrar (OCR)**, under the **Ministry of
Industry, Commerce and Supplies**, registers companies and enforces the Act.

## Types of company

| Type | Key features |
| --- | --- |
| **Private company** | **1 to 101 shareholders**. **Cannot** offer shares to the public. Shares are **restricted** from free transfer. A **one-person company** is allowed |
| **Public company** | At least **7 promoters**. Minimum **paid-up capital Rs 1 crore (10 million)**. **Can** offer shares to the public, with SEBON's approval. Shares are **freely transferable** |
| **Not-for-profit company** | Formed for social, educational, cultural or charitable purposes. Profits **cannot be distributed** |

A private company can **convert** into a public company, and the other way
round, by following the Act's procedure.

## Incorporation documents

| Document | Nepali | What it contains |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Memorandum of Association (MoA)** | प्रबन्धपत्र | The company's **charter**: name, registered office, **objectives**, authorised, issued and paid-up **capital**, and details of shares. It sets the limits of what the company may do |
| **Articles of Association (AoA)** | नियमावली | The **internal rules**: meetings, directors' appointments and powers, share transfers and other procedures |
| **Prospectus** | विवरणपत्र | Required when a **public** company invites the public to buy shares; it must be approved by **SEBON** |

The MoA sits **above** the AoA. If they conflict, the **MoA prevails**. An act
outside the MoA's objectives is **ultra vires**, which means beyond the
company's powers, and is void.

## Shares and capital

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Authorised capital** | The maximum capital the company may issue, as stated in the MoA |
| **Issued capital** | The part offered to shareholders |
| **Paid-up capital** | The part actually **paid** by shareholders |

- Companies can issue **ordinary shares**, **preference shares**, **bonus
  shares** and **rights shares**, and can **buy back** shares under conditions.
- **Debentures** are borrowings, not share capital.

## Meetings

| Meeting | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| **First AGM** | Within **one year** of the company starting business |
| **Annual General Meeting (AGM)** | Every year, within **six months** of the end of the financial year |
| **Special General Meeting (SGM)** | Called when needed, by the board or on shareholders' request |

**Ordinary resolutions** pass by a **simple majority**. **Special
resolutions** are needed for major changes, such as **amending the MoA or
AoA**, a **merger** or **voluntary liquidation**, and need a **larger
majority**.

The AGM:

- approves the **annual accounts**;
- appoints the **auditor**;
- declares the **dividend**;
- elects **directors**.

## Directors and management

- A **public company's** board can have **up to 11 directors**.
- Directors have duties of **loyalty, care and disclosure**. They must
  **declare any personal interest** in the company's transactions.
- **Loans to directors** and **related-party transactions** are restricted.
- The **managing director** or **chief executive** runs the company day to
  day, under the board.
- A **company secretary** is required for larger public companies.

## Accounts and audit

- Every company must keep proper **books of account**, and prepare **annual
  financial statements** following the **Nepal Financial Reporting Standards
  (NFRS)**.
- A **registered auditor** must **audit** the accounts every year.
- Companies file their **annual returns and accounts** with the **OCR**.

## Mergers and winding up

- Companies can **merge** with approval by special resolution and from the
  regulator where one applies.
- **Winding up** (liquidation) can be **voluntary**, by a special resolution of
  the shareholders, or **compulsory**, by court order. An **insolvent**
  company is dealt with under the **Insolvency Act, 2063**.

## How it applies to banks

- Every BFI must be a **public company** registered under this Act.
- The **[Bank and Financial Institution Act, 2073](/docs/bafia-2073)** is the
  **special law** for BFIs. **Where BAFIA has a specific rule, it prevails**,
  and the Companies Act fills the gaps.
- **Rastriya Banijya Bank** is a public company that is **majority-owned by
  the Government of Nepal**.

## Quick revision

> [!TIP]
> **One-line answers.** The **Companies Act, 2063** replaced the **2053**
> Act and is administered by the **OCR**. A **private** company has **1–101**
> shareholders. A **public** company needs at least **7 promoters** and **Rs 1
> crore** paid-up capital. **MoA** = charter and objectives; **AoA** = internal
> rules. AGM within **six months** of the year-end. A public company may have
> **up to 11 directors**. For banks, **BAFIA prevails** over the Companies Act.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Traps that show up in multiple-choice questions: the **MoA prevails** over
> the AoA; an act beyond the MoA is **ultra vires** and void; a **private**
> company **cannot** issue shares to the public; a **one-person** private
> company is allowed; **debentures** are **debt**, not share capital.
