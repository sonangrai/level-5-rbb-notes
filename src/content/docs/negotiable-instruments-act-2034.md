This note covers syllabus item **3.6 Negotiable Instruments Act, 2034**.

The **Negotiable Instruments Act, 2034 (1977)** governs documents that promise
or order **payment of money** and can be **transferred** from one person to
another. It has been amended several times since.

## What is a negotiable instrument?

A **negotiable instrument** is a **written document** with these features:

- It contains an **unconditional promise or order** to pay a **definite sum
  of money**.
- It is **freely transferable**, either by **delivery** alone or by
  **endorsement and delivery**.
- A person who receives it **in good faith and for value**, the **holder in
  due course**, gets a **good title**, even if an earlier holder's title was
  defective.

## The three negotiable instruments

| Instrument | Nature | Parties |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Promissory note** | A written **promise** by one person to pay another | **Maker** (promises) and **payee** (receives) |
| **Bill of exchange** | A written **order** by one person telling another to pay a third person | **Drawer** (orders), **drawee** (ordered to pay) and **payee** |
| **Cheque** | A bill of exchange drawn on a **specified bank**, payable **on demand** | **Drawer** (account holder), **drawee** (bank) and **payee** |

> [!NOTE]
> Every **cheque** is a **bill of exchange**, but not every bill of exchange is
> a cheque. A cheque is always drawn **on a bank** and payable **on demand**.

## Parties to a negotiable instrument

| Party | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Drawer / maker** | The person who writes and signs the instrument |
| **Drawee** | The person or bank ordered to pay |
| **Payee** | The person named to receive payment |
| **Holder** | The person entitled to possess the instrument and receive payment |
| **Holder in due course** | A holder who took the instrument **for value**, **in good faith**, **before it became due**, and **without knowing of any defect** in title |
| **Endorser** | A holder who signs the instrument to transfer it |
| **Endorsee** | The person to whom it is endorsed |
| **Acceptor** | A drawee who has **accepted** a bill, agreeing to pay it |

## Negotiation and endorsement

**Negotiation** means transferring an instrument so that the receiver becomes
its **holder**:

- A **bearer** instrument is negotiated **by delivery** alone.
- An **order** instrument is negotiated **by endorsement and delivery**.

**Endorsement** means the holder **signing** the instrument, usually on the
back, to transfer it. There are several kinds:

| Type | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Blank (general)** | The endorser **only signs**. The instrument becomes **payable to bearer** |
| **Special (full)** | Names the endorsee: "Pay to X or order" |
| **Restrictive** | **Stops further negotiation**: "Pay to X only" |
| **Conditional** | Payment depends on a condition |
| **Sans recourse** | The endorser **excludes personal liability** if the instrument is dishonoured |

## Crossing of cheques

**Crossing** means drawing **two parallel lines** across the face of a cheque.
A crossed cheque **cannot be paid in cash** over the counter. It must be paid
**into a bank account**. This makes it **safer**, because the money can be
traced.

| Type | Form | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **General crossing** | Two parallel lines, with or without "& Co." | Paid only **through a bank account** |
| **Special crossing** | A **bank's name** written between the lines | Paid only **through that bank** |
| **Account payee crossing** | "**A/C Payee**" written between the lines | Credited only to the **payee's own account** |
| **Not negotiable crossing** | "**Not negotiable**" written between the lines | Can be transferred, but the receiver gets **no better title** than the transferor had |

## Presentment, payment and dishonour

### Presentment

- A cheque must be **presented** to the drawee bank for payment **within its
  validity period**. In Nepal, a cheque is valid for **six months** from its
  date.
- A cheque presented after this period is **stale**, and the bank will refuse
  to pay it.

### Dishonour

An instrument is **dishonoured** when the drawee **refuses to accept it** (a
bill) or **refuses to pay it**. Common reasons for a cheque:

- **Not enough funds** in the account.
- The **signature does not match**.
- The cheque is **stale**, or **post-dated** and presented early.
- The drawer has **stopped payment**.
- The account is **closed or frozen**.
- The cheque has been **altered**, or is **damaged or unclear**.

When an instrument is dishonoured:

- The holder must give **notice of dishonour** to the drawer and endorsers to
  keep them **liable**.
- For bills of exchange, **noting and protest** by a notary formally records
  the dishonour.
- **A cheque bounced for lack of funds is a punishable offence.** The drawer
  can face a **fine, imprisonment, or both**, and must **pay the cheque
  amount**. Action can also be taken under the
  [Banking Offence and Punishment Act](/docs/money-laundering-banking-offence-acts).

## The paying bank's duties and protection

- The bank must **pay a valid cheque** when the account has enough funds. If it
  **wrongly refuses**, the customer can claim **damages**.
- It must **check the signature** and look for any **material alteration**.
- It must **follow the crossing** and any **stop-payment instruction**.
- A bank that pays **in good faith**, **without negligence** and **in the
  ordinary course of business** is **protected** even if the instrument later
  turns out to be forged by an endorser.

## Quick revision

> [!TIP]
> **One-line answers.** The **NI Act, 2034 (1977)** covers three instruments:
> **promissory note** (a **promise**; two parties), **bill of exchange** (an
> **order**; three parties) and **cheque** (a bill **drawn on a bank**,
> payable **on demand**). A **holder in due course** takes the instrument
> **for value, in good faith, before maturity, without notice of defects**. A
> **blank** endorsement makes it payable to **bearer**. **A/C Payee** means
> credited only to the payee's account. A cheque is valid for **six months**.
> A **cheque without funds** is a **punishable offence**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Traps that show up in multiple-choice questions: a **promissory note** has
> **no drawee**; a **"Not negotiable"** crossing does **not** stop transfer,
> it only limits the title the receiver gets; a **restrictive** endorsement
> stops further negotiation; a **sans recourse** endorser is **not liable** on
> dishonour; a **bearer** instrument needs **no endorsement** to transfer.
