This note covers syllabus items **4.3 Communication: Skill, Approach** and
**4.4 Team Building and Workmanship**. They are closely linked: **a team works
only as well as its members communicate**.

## Communication

**Communication** is the process of **sharing information, ideas and
feelings** so that the receiver **understands** them as the sender meant.
Communication is complete only when the message is **understood**, not just
sent.

### The communication process

```text
Sender → Encoding → Message → Channel → Decoding → Receiver
   ↑                                                   │
   └──────────────────── Feedback ─────────────────────┘
                 (noise can interfere at every step)
```

| Element | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Sender** | Has the idea and starts the communication |
| **Encoding** | Turning the idea into words, symbols or gestures |
| **Message** | What is actually sent |
| **Channel** | The medium: face to face, phone, email, letter, notice |
| **Decoding** | The receiver **interpreting** the message |
| **Receiver** | The person the message is for |
| **Feedback** | The receiver's response, which shows whether the message was understood |
| **Noise** | Anything that **distorts** the message, such as physical noise, jargon or emotion |

### Types of communication

| Basis | Types |
| --- | --- |
| **Medium** | **Verbal**: oral or written. **Non-verbal**: body language, facial expression, tone, gestures |
| **Direction** | **Downward**: superior to subordinate, such as orders and policies. **Upward**: subordinate to superior, such as reports, suggestions and complaints. **Horizontal (lateral)**: between peers. **Diagonal**: across levels and departments |
| **Formality** | **Formal**: through official channels. **Informal**: the **grapevine**, which is fast but can spread rumours |

### Barriers to communication

| Barrier | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| **Physical** | Noise, distance, poor network, bad equipment |
| **Semantic** (language) | Jargon, ambiguous words, different languages |
| **Psychological** | Emotions, stress, prejudice, distrust, poor attention |
| **Organisational** | Too many levels, rigid hierarchy, unclear policies |
| **Cultural** | Different norms, customs and meanings of gestures |
| **Information overload** | Too many messages, so important ones are missed |

### The 7 Cs of effective communication

1. **Clear**: one idea, plainly stated.
2. **Concise**: no unnecessary words.
3. **Concrete**: specific facts and figures.
4. **Correct**: accurate and free of errors.
5. **Coherent**: logical and well-connected.
6. **Complete**: everything the receiver needs.
7. **Courteous**: polite and respectful.

### Communication skills

- **Active listening**: giving full attention, not interrupting, asking
  questions and **paraphrasing** to check understanding.
- **Speaking**: clear voice, simple words, suitable tone.
- **Writing**: good letters, emails, reports and minutes, structured with the
  main point first.
- **Non-verbal skills**: eye contact, open posture, a calm tone.
- **Presentation skills**: planning, clear structure, visual aids.
- **Empathy**: understanding the other person's point of view.
- **Giving feedback**: specific, timely and about **behaviour**, not the
  person.

### Communication approaches (styles)

| Style | Behaviour | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Passive** | Avoids saying what they think; gives in | Their own needs go unmet; resentment |
| **Aggressive** | Forceful and blaming; ignores others' rights | Conflict and fear |
| **Passive-aggressive** | Agrees openly but resists indirectly | Distrust |
| **Assertive** | States needs and views **honestly and respectfully** | **Most effective**: respect on both sides |

### The Johari window

The **Johari window** is a model of **self-awareness** in communication:

| | Known to self | Not known to self |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Known to others** | **Open** area | **Blind** area |
| **Not known to others** | **Hidden** area | **Unknown** area |

Communication improves as the **open area grows**: through **self-disclosure**,
which shrinks the hidden area, and **feedback**, which shrinks the blind area.

## Team building

### Group vs team

| Point | Group | Team |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Goal | Individual goals | A **shared goal** |
| Accountability | **Individual** | **Individual and mutual** |
| Skills | Random or similar | **Complementary** |
| Result | Sum of individual work | **Synergy**: more than the sum of individual work |

### Tuckman's stages of team development (1965)

| Stage | What happens | The leader should |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Forming** | Members are polite and unsure, and rely on the leader | Give **direction** and clear roles |
| **Storming** | **Conflict** over roles, ideas and leadership | **Manage conflict**, encourage open talk |
| **Norming** | Rules, roles and trust are agreed; **cohesion** grows | **Support** and facilitate |
| **Performing** | The team works **independently and effectively** | **Delegate** and step back |
| **Adjourning** | The task ends and the team disbands (added in **1977**) | **Recognise** the team's work |

### Characteristics of an effective team

- A **clear, shared goal**.
- **Defined roles** and **complementary skills**.
- **Trust** and **open communication**.
- **Healthy conflict**: disagreements about ideas, not personalities.
- **Mutual accountability**.
- **Supportive leadership**.
- **Recognition** of the team's success.

### Team-building activities

- **Clear goal-setting** together.
- **Joint problem-solving** and brainstorming.
- **Cross-training** and **job rotation**.
- **Celebrating** achievements together.
- **Off-site** retreats and team activities.
- **Regular meetings** and feedback.

### Problems in teams

- **Groupthink**: the wish for **agreement** overrides critical thinking, which
  leads to bad decisions.
- **Social loafing**: some members **make less effort** because they think
  others will do the work.
- **Unresolved conflict** and **poor leadership**.

## Workmanship

**Workmanship** is the **quality, skill and care** a person puts into their
work. In a bank, good workmanship means:

- **Accuracy**: correct entries, calculations and documentation.
- **Diligence**: following procedures fully, for example in KYC and loan
  documentation.
- **Timeliness**: meeting deadlines and service standards.
- **Integrity and confidentiality**: honesty, and protecting customer
  information.
- **Ownership**: taking responsibility for one's work and its results.
- **Continuous learning**: keeping up with new products, rules and
  technology.
- **Professional behaviour**: courtesy, punctuality and the right appearance.

Good workmanship is the **result** of good teamwork and motivation: people
take pride in their work when they feel valued and part of a team.

## Quick revision

> [!TIP]
> **One-line answers.** The communication process runs **sender → encoding →
> channel → decoding → receiver → feedback**, and **noise** can interfere at
> any step. The **7 Cs** are clear, concise, concrete, correct, coherent,
> complete and courteous. **Assertive** communication is the most effective
> style. **Johari window**: open, blind, hidden, unknown. **Tuckman**:
> **forming, storming, norming, performing, adjourning**. A **team** has a
> shared goal and **mutual accountability**; a group does not.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Traps that show up in multiple-choice questions: communication is complete
> only when there is **feedback**; the **grapevine** is **informal**
> communication; **storming** is the **conflict** stage; **adjourning** was
> added **later (1977)**; **groupthink** is **too much** agreement, not
> conflict; **social loafing** means **reduced effort** in a group.
