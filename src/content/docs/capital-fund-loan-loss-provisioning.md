This note covers syllabus items **2.1 Capital Fund** and **2.2 Loan Loss
Provisioning**. They belong together because both protect a bank against
**losses on its loans**:

- **Provisions** cover the losses a bank **expects**. They are set aside from
  profit as soon as a loan goes bad.
- **Capital** absorbs the losses it **does not expect**. It is the owners' money
  that stands between losses and depositors.

## Capital fund

A bank's **capital fund** is the owners' money plus certain reserves and
long-term instruments that can **absorb losses** without the bank failing.
Depositors are protected because shareholders lose their money first.

NRB regulates capital through the **Capital Adequacy Framework**. For
commercial banks it is based on **Basel III**, and the rules are published in
the **Unified Directives**.

### Components

| Tier | Also called | Includes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Tier 1** | Core capital | **Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1)**: paid-up capital, share premium, statutory general reserve, retained earnings and other free reserves. **Additional Tier 1 (AT1)**: perpetual instruments that meet Basel III conditions |
| **Tier 2** | Supplementary capital | Subordinated term debt (within limits), **general loan loss provision** on pass loans (up to **1.25% of credit risk-weighted exposure**), exchange fluctuation fund, investment adjustment reserve and similar reserves |

Some items are **deducted** from Tier 1 because they cannot absorb losses:
**goodwill, deferred tax assets, fictitious assets**, and investments in other
financial institutions' capital beyond set limits.

> [!NOTE]
> **Statutory general reserve:** BAFIA requires a BFI to move **20% of its net
> profit** into the general reserve **every year** until the reserve equals
> **twice its paid-up capital**. It is part of core capital.

### Capital adequacy ratio (CAR)

The **capital adequacy ratio** compares capital with how risky the bank's
assets are:

> **CAR = Capital fund ÷ Total risk-weighted exposures × 100**

**Risk-weighted exposures** add up three kinds of risk:

- **Credit risk**: each asset is weighted by how risky it is. For example,
  cash and government securities carry **0%**, while an ordinary loan carries
  about **100%** or more.
- **Operational risk**: losses from failed processes, people, systems or
  outside events.
- **Market risk**: losses from changes in exchange rates, interest rates or
  prices.

### Minimum requirements for commercial banks

| Ratio | Minimum |
| --- | --- |
| Common Equity Tier 1 | **4.5%** |
| Tier 1 capital | **6%** |
| Total capital | **8.5%** |
| Capital conservation buffer (CCB, held in CET1) | **2.5%** |
| **Total capital including CCB** | **11%** |
| Countercyclical buffer | Up to **2.5%**, switched on by NRB when needed |
| Leverage ratio (Tier 1 ÷ total exposure, not risk-weighted) | **4%** |

Development banks and finance companies follow their own capital rules, also
set in the Unified Directives.

### The three pillars of Basel

1. **Pillar 1: minimum capital requirements.** Capital is held against credit,
   operational and market risk.
2. **Pillar 2: supervisory review.** The bank runs its own **Internal Capital
   Adequacy Assessment Process (ICAAP)**, and NRB reviews it.
3. **Pillar 3: market discipline.** The bank **discloses** its capital and
   risk publicly, so that the market can judge it.

## Loan loss provisioning

A **loan loss provision** is an amount set aside **from profit** to cover
loans that may not be repaid. The worse a loan's condition, the larger the
provision.

### Loan classification and provision rates

NRB classifies loans by **how long the principal or interest has been
overdue**:

| Category | Overdue period | Minimum provision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Pass** | Not overdue, or overdue **up to 1 month** | **1.25%** |
| **Watch list** | Overdue **1 to 3 months** | **5%** |
| **Substandard** | Overdue **3 to 6 months** | **25%** |
| **Doubtful** | Overdue **6 months to 1 year** | **50%** |
| **Loss** | Overdue **more than 1 year** | **100%** |

- **Performing loans** are Pass and Watch list.
- **Non-performing loans (NPLs)** are Substandard, Doubtful and Loss.
- The **NPL ratio** is non-performing loans divided by total loans.

Provisioning also has these rules:

- **General vs specific provision.** The provision on performing loans is a
  **general provision** and counts as **Tier 2 capital**. The provision on
  NPLs is a **specific provision** against a known problem.
- **Loans go straight to Loss** in some cases, however short the overdue
  period. Examples: the loan was **used for a purpose other than the one it
  was approved for**, the borrower has been **blacklisted**, the borrower has
  **disappeared or been declared bankrupt**, or the **collateral is missing or
  not enough**.
- **Rescheduled or restructured loans** need a provision of at least
  **12.5%**. See
  [Credit Management Terms](/docs/credit-management-terms).
- **NFRS 9.** Under the accounting standard, banks also estimate an
  **expected credit loss (ECL)**. They provide for **whichever is higher**:
  NRB's regulatory provision or the ECL.

### Why provisioning matters

- The bank's **profit and assets are shown truthfully**, because losses are
  recognised early.
- Profit is not **inflated** and then paid out as dividends.
- The **cushion against bad loans** is built up while the bank is still
  earning.

> [!WARNING]
> NRB revises the overdue periods and provision rates in its **Unified
> Directives** from time to time. The **Watch list** category and the
> **1.25%** pass-loan rate are recent changes. Check the latest directive before
> the exam.

## Quick revision

> [!TIP]
> **One-line answers.** Capital fund = **Tier 1** (core) + **Tier 2**
> (supplementary). CAR = capital ÷ risk-weighted exposures. Commercial banks:
> **CET1 4.5%, Tier 1 6%, total 8.5% + 2.5% CCB = 11%**; leverage ratio
> **4%**. General reserve: **20% of net profit** until it equals **2× paid-up
> capital**. Loan classes: **Pass / Watch list / Substandard / Doubtful /
> Loss** with provisions of **1.25 / 5 / 25 / 50 / 100%**. Rescheduled or
> restructured loans: at least **12.5%**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Traps that show up in multiple-choice questions: **NPLs** are only
> **Substandard, Doubtful and Loss**, not Watch list; the **general
> provision** counts as **Tier 2**, not Tier 1; **goodwill and deferred tax
> assets** are **deducted** from capital; the leverage ratio is **not**
> risk-weighted; **ICAAP** belongs to **Pillar 2**; **disclosure** is
> **Pillar 3**.
