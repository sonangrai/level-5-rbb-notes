This note covers syllabus item **4.1 Job Description, Terms of Reference and
Responsibilities**. All three answer the same question in different settings:
**who is expected to do what**.

- A **job description** defines a **permanent position**.
- **Terms of reference (ToR)** define a **committee, project or temporary
  assignment**.
- **Responsibilities** are the duties a person must carry out in either
  setting.

## Job analysis

**Job analysis** is the process of collecting and studying information about a
job: its **tasks, duties and working conditions**, and the **skills** needed to
do it. It produces two documents:

```text
                 Job analysis
                /            \
   Job description        Job specification
   (about the JOB)        (about the PERSON)
```

Common methods of job analysis:

- **Observation** of people doing the job.
- **Interviews** with job holders and supervisors.
- **Questionnaires**.
- **Work diaries or logs** kept by job holders.

## Job description

A **job description (JD)** is a written statement of **what a job involves**.

### Contents

| Element | Example for a bank customer service officer |
| --- | --- |
| **Job title and grade** | Customer Service Officer, Assistant level |
| **Department and location** | Operations, branch office |
| **Reports to** | Branch Operations In-charge |
| **Job summary** | One or two lines on why the job exists |
| **Duties and responsibilities** | Open accounts, complete KYC, handle customer queries, issue cheque books and cards |
| **Authority** | Limits on what the holder may approve |
| **Working conditions** | Hours, shifts, travel |
| **Performance standards** | **Key Result Areas (KRAs)** and **Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)** |

### Uses

- **Recruitment and selection**: tells candidates and interviewers what the
  job is.
- **Performance appraisal**: provides the standard to measure work against.
- **Training needs**: shows what the job holder must be able to do.
- **Job evaluation and pay**: helps set grades and salaries fairly.
- **Clarity**: reduces **overlap, gaps and conflict** between jobs.

## Job specification

A **job specification** describes the **person needed** to do the job well:

- **Education and qualifications**, such as a bachelor's degree in management.
- **Experience**.
- **Skills**: technical, computer, communication.
- **Personal qualities**: integrity, customer orientation, teamwork.
- **Physical or other requirements**, where relevant.

| Point | Job description | Job specification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Describes | The **job** | The **person** |
| Answers | **What** is done and **how** | **Who** can do it |
| Contents | Duties, responsibilities, conditions | Qualifications, skills, experience, qualities |
| Main use | Defining work, appraisal | Recruitment and selection |

## Terms of reference (ToR)

**Terms of reference** define the **purpose, scope and working rules** of a
**committee, task force, project, consultant or study**. A job description
covers an ongoing position; a ToR covers a specific, often **temporary**,
piece of work.

### Typical contents

1. **Background**: why the work is needed.
2. **Objectives**: what it must achieve.
3. **Scope**: what is **included and excluded**.
4. **Composition or team**: members, chair and secretary.
5. **Tasks and methodology**: how the work will be done.
6. **Deliverables**: reports, recommendations, outputs.
7. **Timeline**: deadlines and milestones.
8. **Authority and resources**: what the group may access, and its budget.
9. **Reporting**: who it reports to, and how often.

**Example:** a bank's board forms a **committee to review its loan recovery
policy**. The ToR sets out the committee's members, its scope (for example,
NPLs above a certain amount), its deliverable (a revised policy draft) and its
deadline (three months).

## Responsibility, authority and accountability

| Concept | Meaning | Flows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Responsibility** | The **duty** to carry out an assigned task | **Downward**: assigned by a superior |
| **Authority** | The **right** to make decisions and give orders to get the task done | **Downward**: delegated by a superior |
| **Accountability** | The duty to **answer for results** | **Upward**: owed to the superior |

### Key principles

- **Parity of authority and responsibility.** Authority should **match**
  responsibility. Responsibility without authority leads to frustration;
  authority without responsibility leads to misuse.
- **Accountability cannot be delegated.** A manager can delegate **authority**,
  but remains **accountable** for the result.
- **Unity of command.** Each employee should report to **one** superior, which
  avoids conflicting orders.

### RACI matrix

A **RACI matrix** makes roles clear for each task:

| Letter | Role |
| --- | --- |
| **R**: Responsible | Does the work |
| **A**: Accountable | Owns the result and signs off. There is only **one** per task |
| **C**: Consulted | Gives input before the work is done |
| **I**: Informed | Is kept updated |

## In banking

- **Delegation of authority** is written down: lending limits, approval limits
  and signing powers are set for each level.
- **Segregation of duties** and **maker–checker** controls depend on clear
  job descriptions. See
  [KYC, Audit and Internal Control](/docs/kyc-audit-internal-control).
- Clear responsibilities make it possible to **fix accountability** when a
  loss or fraud happens.

## Quick revision

> [!TIP]
> **One-line answers.** Job analysis produces the **job description** (about
> the job) and the **job specification** (about the person). A **ToR**
> defines a **committee, project or assignment**: objectives, scope,
> deliverables, timeline and reporting. **Responsibility** and **authority**
> flow **down**; **accountability** flows **up**. Authority should **match**
> responsibility. **Accountability cannot be delegated**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Traps that show up in multiple-choice questions: **qualifications** belong
> in the **job specification**, not the job description; **accountability**
> cannot be delegated, but **authority** can; a RACI chart has only **one
> "A"** per task; a **ToR** is used for **temporary** work, not permanent
> positions.
