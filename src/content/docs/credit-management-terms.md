This note covers syllabus items **2.5 Refinancing, Rescheduling,
Restructuring**, **2.7 Credit Information and Blacklisting** and **2.8 Credit
Ratings**. All three are about **managing credit risk**: checking a borrower
before lending, and dealing with a loan that has gone wrong.

## Refinancing, rescheduling and restructuring

These three words are easy to confuse, so it helps to keep the distinction
clear from the start:

| Term | What changes | Who usually does it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Refinancing** | A **new source of funds** pays for the loan | NRB funding a bank's loan, or a new loan paying off an old one |
| **Rescheduling** | Only the **repayment schedule** | A bank, for a borrower in temporary difficulty |
| **Restructuring** | The **terms and structure** of the loan | A bank, for a borrower in deeper difficulty |

### Refinancing

In Nepal, **refinance** usually means **NRB's refinance facility**:

- A BFI lends to a **priority sector**, such as agriculture, small and
  cottage industries, exports or tourism, at a **low interest rate**.
- It then **borrows the same amount from NRB** at a **concessional rate**.
- This lets NRB send **cheap credit** to the sectors it wants to support,
  through the banks.
- NRB has run **general refinance**, **special refinance** (for example, after
  the **2015 earthquake** and during **COVID-19**) and **export refinance**.

At the **borrower level**, refinancing means **taking a new loan**, often from
another bank, **to repay an existing one**, usually to get a lower rate or
better terms. This is also called a loan **take-over** or swap.

### Rescheduling

**Rescheduling** changes **only when the borrower repays**:

- The **loan period** is extended, or the **instalments** are rearranged.
- The **interest rate and other terms stay the same**.
- It suits a borrower with a **temporary cash-flow problem** whose business
  is still viable.

### Restructuring

**Restructuring** changes the **loan's terms or structure** more deeply. For
example, the bank may:

- change the **interest rate**;
- **convert** one kind of loan into another, such as an overdraft into a term
  loan;
- **combine** several loans, or **split** one loan up;
- change the **repayment plan**, the **collateral**, or both.

It suits a borrower whose business **can survive only if the loan is
changed**.

### NRB conditions for rescheduling and restructuring

- The **borrower** must apply in writing, with a **viable business or
  repayment plan**.
- The borrower usually has to pay a set share of the **interest due** first.
- The bank must **set aside a provision of at least 12.5%** on the loan, and
  keep it until the borrower shows a record of regular repayment.
- **Interest is not booked as income** until it is actually received.
- Rescheduling or restructuring should **not be used to hide bad loans**. NRB
  examines these loans closely.

> [!NOTE]
> NRB has sometimes allowed **relaxed rescheduling or restructuring** for
> sectors hit by a shock, such as tourism and hotels during **COVID-19**,
> usually with lower provisioning for a limited period.

## Credit information and blacklisting

### Credit Information Centre (CICL)

The **Credit Information Centre Ltd. (CICL)**, also called *Karja Suchana
Kendra*, keeps a **national database of borrowers**:

- It began in **2046 BS (1989)** as the **Credit Information Bureau**, set up
  on NRB's initiative. It later became a **company**, with NRB as a major
  shareholder.
- **Every BFI must report** its borrowers' loan details to CICL and update
  them regularly.
- **Before approving a loan**, a BFI checks the applicant's **credit
  information report (CIR)**. The report shows the applicant's existing loans
  at other institutions, repayment history and any blacklisting.
- CICL also gives borrowers **credit scores**, which helps banks price risk.

### Why credit information matters

- It stops one borrower from taking **loans at many banks** using the **same
  collateral** or false information.
- It reduces **information asymmetry**: the bank learns the borrower's real
  record.
- It encourages **good repayment**, because a default follows the borrower to
  every bank.

### Blacklisting

A borrower is **blacklisted** when the lending BFI asks CICL to add them to
the **blacklist**, in line with NRB's rules. Typical grounds include:

- **Wilful default**: the borrower can repay but does not.
- The loan was **used for a purpose other than the one it was approved for**.
- The loan was obtained with **false documents** or **fake collateral**.
- The loan has been classified as **Loss**, and recovery action, such as
  auctioning the collateral, has **not recovered** it in full.
- The borrower **cannot be found** or has been declared **bankrupt**.

Blacklisting covers the **borrower** and also the **related people and firms**:
**directors, promoters, major shareholders and guarantors** of a blacklisted
firm, and other firms they control.

### What happens to a blacklisted borrower

- **No new loans** from any BFI, and no new **credit facilities** such as
  guarantees or letters of credit.
- They **cannot be a promoter, director or chief executive** of a BFI.
- Other laws may add more restrictions.

The name is **removed from the blacklist** after the **loan is fully repaid**
and the lending BFI asks CICL to remove it. Removal follows NRB's procedure
and may involve a **waiting period**.

## Credit ratings

A **credit rating** is an **independent opinion** of how likely a borrower,
company or debt security is to **repay on time**. It is shown as a
letter-grade symbol, such as **AAA** (highest safety) down to **D**
(default).

### Credit rating agencies in Nepal

| Agency | Established |
| --- | --- |
| **ICRA Nepal Ltd.** | **2012**, the first credit rating agency in Nepal |
| **CARE Ratings Nepal Ltd.** | **2017** |

- They are **licensed and regulated** by the **Securities Board of Nepal
  (SEBON)** under the **Credit Rating Regulations, 2068 (2011)**.
- Their rating symbols carry a Nepal prefix, for example **[ICRANP] AA**.

### Where ratings are used

- **Public issues**: SEBON requires a credit rating for **IPOs**, **rights
  and further public offers** and **debentures**, so investors can judge the
  risk.
- **Bank risk management**: banks give their own borrowers an **internal
  credit rating** before lending. It decides whether the bank lends, the
  **premium** over the base rate, the collateral required and how closely the
  loan is monitored.
- **Bank ratings**: BFIs obtain their own **institutional ratings** and
  publish them.

### Why ratings matter

- **Investors** get a simple, independent measure of risk.
- **Good borrowers** can raise money **more cheaply**.
- **Market discipline** improves, because a downgrade signals trouble early.

### Limitations

- A rating is an **opinion, not a guarantee**.
- It can be **slow to change** when conditions get worse quickly.
- There is a **conflict of interest**, because the company being rated pays
  for the rating.

## Quick revision

> [!TIP]
> **One-line answers.** **Refinance**: a BFI lends to a priority sector, then
> borrows the same amount from NRB at a concessional rate. **Rescheduling**:
> only the **repayment schedule** changes. **Restructuring**: the **terms or
> structure** change. Both need at least **12.5%** provision. **CICL** keeps
> the national borrower database and the **blacklist**; it began in **2046
> BS**. Rating agencies: **ICRA Nepal (2012)** and **CARE Ratings Nepal
> (2017)**, regulated by **SEBON**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Traps that show up in multiple-choice questions: rescheduling does **not**
> change the interest rate, but restructuring **may**; blacklisting extends to
> **directors, promoters and guarantors**; credit rating agencies are
> regulated by **SEBON, not NRB**; a credit rating is **not** a guarantee of
> repayment; **CIR** stands for **credit information report**.
