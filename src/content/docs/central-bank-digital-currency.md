This note covers syllabus item **6.6 Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC)**.

## What is a CBDC?

A **Central Bank Digital Currency** is a **digital form of a country's
currency**, issued directly by the **central bank**.

- It is **legal tender**, just like banknotes.
- It is a **direct liability of the central bank**, so it carries **no credit
  risk**: it cannot "fail" the way a private company can.
- Think of it as **digital cash**: the safety of a banknote with the
  convenience of a digital payment.

### Why it is different from money in a bank account

When you hold money in a bank account, you hold a **claim on that bank**,
which is a private company. A CBDC is a **claim on the central bank itself**,
just as cash is.

## Types of CBDC

| Basis | Types |
| --- | --- |
| **Who uses it** | **Retail CBDC**: for the **general public** and everyday payments. **Wholesale CBDC**: only for **banks and financial institutions**, to settle interbank and large payments |
| **How ownership is recorded** | **Account-based**: ownership is recorded in an **account**, and the **user's identity** is checked. **Token-based**: whoever **holds the token** owns it, like **cash**, and the token's validity is checked |
| **How it is distributed** | **Direct**: the central bank deals with the public itself. **Two-tier (intermediated)**: the central bank issues CBDC, and **banks and PSPs distribute** it to the public. This is the **most common** model |

## Why central banks consider a CBDC

- **Cash use is falling**, so CBDC keeps **public access to central bank
  money**.
- **Cheaper and more efficient payments**, including **offline** payments.
- **Financial inclusion**: reaching people without bank accounts.
- **Competition** for **private digital currencies** and **stablecoins**.
- **Faster, cheaper cross-border payments**.
- **Monetary sovereignty**: the country keeps control over its own money.
- **Less cost** of printing, transporting and handling cash.

## Risks and challenges

- **Bank disintermediation**: if people move deposits from banks into CBDC,
  banks have **less money to lend**. Holding limits can reduce this.
- **Bank runs**: in a crisis, people could move money into CBDC **very
  quickly**.
- **Privacy**: a central bank could see **every transaction**.
- **Cyber security**: the system would be a **critical target**.
- **Technology and cost**: building and running it is complex and expensive.
- **Digital divide**: people without phones, internet or digital skills.

## Comparing CBDC with other forms of money

| Point | CBDC | Cash | Bank deposit | Wallet balance | Cryptocurrency |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Issuer | **Central bank** | Central bank | Commercial bank | PSP (backed by bank deposits) | **No central issuer** |
| Legal tender | **Yes** | Yes | No | No | **No** |
| Credit risk | **None** | None | Bank risk | PSP and bank risk | Very high price risk |
| Form | Digital | Physical | Digital | Digital | Digital |
| Legal status in Nepal | Not issued yet | Legal | Legal | Legal | **Illegal** |

> [!NOTE]
> A CBDC may use **blockchain** technology, but it is **not a
> cryptocurrency**. It is **centrally issued and controlled**, and its value is
> **always equal** to the national currency, so 1 digital rupee = 1 rupee.

## Examples from around the world

| Country | CBDC | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Bahamas** | **Sand Dollar** | **First** nationwide retail CBDC, launched in **October 2020** |
| **Nigeria** | **eNaira** | Launched in **2021** |
| **Jamaica** | **JAM-DEX** | Launched in **2022** |
| **China** | **e-CNY** (digital yuan) | Large-scale **pilot** |
| **India** | **e₹ (digital rupee)** | **Pilots** since **2022**, both wholesale and retail |

Many central banks, including the US Federal Reserve, the European Central
Bank and the Bank of England, are **researching** CBDCs or running **pilots**.

## CBDC in Nepal

- **NRB** has been **studying** CBDC and its effect on Nepal's payment system,
  banks and monetary policy. It has mentioned CBDC research in its **Monetary
  Policy** statements.
- **Nepal has not issued a CBDC.**
- **Cryptocurrency** remains **illegal** in Nepal. A CBDC would be the
  **legal**, central-bank-backed alternative.

> [!WARNING]
> NRB's position on CBDC is changing. Check the **latest Monetary Policy** and
> any **NRB concept paper** on CBDC for its current stage before the exam.

## Quick revision

> [!TIP]
> **One-line answers.** A **CBDC** is digital money issued by the **central
> bank**: **legal tender** and a **central bank liability**. **Retail** CBDC
> is for the public; **wholesale** CBDC is for banks. **Account-based**
> records identity; **token-based** works like cash. The **two-tier** model,
> where banks distribute it, is the most common. The **Bahamas Sand Dollar**
> (**2020**) was the **first**. **India's e₹** has been piloted since
> **2022**. **Nepal**: NRB is studying CBDC; **none has been issued**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Traps that show up in multiple-choice questions: a CBDC is **not a
> cryptocurrency**; **wholesale** CBDC is **not** for the public; the main
> risk to banks is **disintermediation**; a **wallet balance** is **not**
> central bank money; a CBDC's value is **fixed** to the national currency.
